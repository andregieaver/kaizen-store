# Kaizen Store against Shopify: feature parity

Research date: October 2026. Written on 2026-10-02 from Shopify's documentation and a read of the code, then re-read row by row on 2026-10-03 for the tracker: Shopify's own pages were fetched where they could be, and each Kaizen rating was checked against the code and the tests its row lists.

**The tables in this report are generated.** The data is `docs/parity/rows/*.json` (the contract and its rules are in `docs/parity/README.md`). `pnpm parity` prints the figures, `pnpm parity:write` rewrites everything between the `parity:generated` markers, and `pnpm parity:check` (and a unit test) fails when the report and the data disagree. Change a row, not a table; the prose around the tables is written by hand.

## 1. The answer

<!-- parity:generated:start summary -->

Kaizena Flow has **75.3%** weighted parity with what Shopify ships natively on its Basic, Grow and Advanced plans, across 215 core features weighted by importance to an EU small or mid-size merchant, and **83.9%** on the must-haves (the 89 features with weight 4 or 5). The unweighted figures are 71.9% overall and 83.7% for must-haves. Counting app-only features gives 74.9%; counting Plus-only as well gives 74.4%. Kaizena Flow is strongest on storefront, themes and content (86.6%) and weakest on platform, extensibility and operations (54.7%), ai and automation (66.9%), checkout and payments (68.2%). Of the 215 core features, 110 are full, 89 partial and 16 missing, so half-built features, not absent ones, are the main shape of the gap.

<!-- parity:generated:end summary -->

The method is simple: a full match scores 1, a partial match 0.5, a missing feature 0, each multiplied by a weight from 1 to 5. The weights are my judgement, not data, and a different owner would set some differently (the unweighted figures beside the weighted ones show how much that matters). The number measures how much of Shopify's native feature set Kaizen has caught up with. It does not measure where Kaizen is better (bookings, EU compliance, AI and analytics honesty, section 4), and it excludes anything Shopify only offers on Plus or through the App Store. That exclusion flatters Kaizen: Shopify's App Store (commonly cited at 13,000+ apps; one 2026 tracker puts public apps at 18k to 25k) fills most remaining gaps for a Shopify merchant, while Kaizen has no app model at all, so the percentage understates the real gap in extensibility.

## 6. Caveats

- **Research date.** The Shopify side reflects pages read in October 2026 (including the Summer '26 Edition). Shopify changes plan limits and app availability often, and some claims come from secondary sources and recaps, which the row's text marks as secondary.
- **Shopify side not fetched.** A row whose Shopify page could not be read keeps `fetched: false` and says why in its text; the evidence table above counts them. The first report had 36 rows resting on memory; the re-read of 2026-10-03 fetched them and shows what is left.
- **Weights are judgement.** Each row's 1 to 5 weight reflects an EU small or mid-size merchant, and the tracker's rules forbid changing weights in a wave that also changes ratings. The unweighted figures in the headline table show how far the weights move the result: a few points, not tens.
- **Rechecks lifted the score, then a re-read lowered it.** In the first report 15 rows were rechecked by a skeptic: 14 went up (mostly from missing to partial) and 1 went down, and by its own arithmetic the headline would have been roughly 57% without them. Since then every row has been re-read and every move is in the row's history (listed at the top). Several raised rows still say in their own text that "partial leaning missing" is the honest reading (the Merchant Center feed, AI-written marketing email, 3PL), so the true figure may sit lower than the headline.
- **Cross-domain double counting and disagreement.** The same Shopify feature is a row in more than one domain (discount codes, gift cards, the Merchant Center feed, URL redirects, metafields, blog, cookie consent, B2B, draft orders, search merchandising, the admin AI assistant, withdrawal), so the core rows include repeats and the overall figure leans on them. Some repeats are rated or weighted differently (when this was written: discount codes, the Merchant Center feed, the admin AI assistant and search merchandising). Each rating is kept as researched, and a wave that moves one row should move its twins; the tracker does not reconcile them.
- **A Full row is held by tests, not proved by them.** The tracker checks that the files and test files a Full row lists exist and counts the rows that say "untested"; it does not run the tests (CI does) and cannot tell a good test from a weak one. Carrier and payment features are built but unproven against live carrier and Stripe services.
- **D152 and D153 are shipped and counted as built.** The Analytics domain and several Customers, Orders and AI rows rest on them. Visit counting is off by default, so conversion, funnel and channel figures exist only for stores that switch it on.
- **Not counted in the headline.** Shopify's App Store ecosystem itself (only the app-only rows that stand in for it appear, in the "core plus app-only" line) and Plus-only features (the last line). POS and the Shop app are native rows and are counted, as missing. For a merchant who would simply install apps on Shopify, Kaizen's real gap is wider than the headline suggests.
- **"Where Kaizen is ahead" claims are unscored.** Doc-only items in section 4 were not verified on the Shopify side, and several source claims ("Shopify has no native X") are contradicted or narrowed by the rated rows, as noted there.

## 7. Suggested build order

Ordered by legal exposure first, then by how many gaps one piece of work closes; the full plan, with its waves and the decisions it needs, is `docs/parity-plan.md`, and the score each wave would reach is in the projection above. Sizes are rough and are my guess, not from the research.

1. **Compliance and money** (wave 1). The withdrawal function and returns flow shipped as D153. What remains of the legal items: human-reviewed starter policy pages with a terms-acceptance link at checkout, VAT completeness and reporting (editable reduced-rate categories, a VAT-by-country and rate report with export for OSS filing, EU VAT ID check with reverse charge), shop-order invoices and credit notes in Kaizen's own gap-free series (the Work module already has the machinery), unit price, GDPR export and erasure, and staff two-step authentication and roles.
2. **Data in and out** (wave 2). One import and export pipeline for products, orders and customers, bulk product editing, and automatic redirects when a handle changes. This is also the migration path for any merchant arriving from Shopify or WooCommerce.
3. **Inventory and order operations** (wave 3). An inventory page with adjustments and reasons, backorders, a locations screen, order search with tags and bulk actions, draft orders, shipping rate tables and local pickup.
4. **Payments breadth** (wave 4). Show and request local methods (iDEAL, Bancontact, BLIK and so on) from Kaizen's own admin, add dispute and payout handling to the webhook, and decide whether a second provider adapter (Mollie or Adyen) is needed given that Stripe blocks PayPal for direct-charge platforms. Add Swish and Vipps when Stripe opens them.
5. **Customers and marketing** (wave 5). A subscriber list with consent, broadcast email to a saved segment, a segment builder, the Merchant Center feed, richer discount-code scope and product reviews.
6. **Catalogue, storefront and international, analytics and AI** (waves 6 to 8). The variant limit, smart collections and bundles; speed report, per-market prices and customs; the report builder and the assistant's missing editing tools.
7. **Platform trust and reach** (wave 9). A status page, a public API with tokens and open webhooks, and self-serve sign-up. The API and webhooks are the first step toward any extension model, which is where Shopify's largest advantage sits.
