# Grade A for: The database

Part of `docs/audits/bloat-review-2026-11.md`. **Today: C.** C stands, made of two halves. The shape is close to a B: one small pool, statement and lock limits, queues claimed with skip locked, triggers gated by column, migration safety and the typed-read ratchet held by tests. The asking side is close to a D: every cold page fetches all of a store's pages to find one, 122 store filters defeat their index (about 15 million rows scanned a day), 75% of transactions change nothing, pg_cron's log is 77% of the database, and nothing counts a statement or a row. It is not an E: the listed app statements cost about 30 s of database time a day, and nobody is hurt yet.

**The bar, in a sentence.** A read or a scheduled run costs what the asking store holds and what has changed, never what the whole platform holds. No statement fetches a document it does not draw, asks twice, writes nothing or runs slow, and a test or a daily production reading holds each of these numbers.

## An A lives with

- Postgres reads a table of a few pages whole by choice. Tables under 500 rows are not judged, and a daily prune, a backup and the daily usage count each read a table once.
- A job over all stores reads each store's row: at most 10 store rows per added store a tick. At 6,000 stores that is a fraction of a second every five minutes.
- An idle tick still sends up to 125 small statements, about 36,000 a day, and each gated route keeps a 15-minute net of 96 runs a day. The totals are the scheduled-work area's bar.
- orders keeps 32 indexes and 27 triggers until eight weeks of real traffic can judge them. None is provably redundant today, and each new one needs a written reason.
- The 414 indexes on foreign keys and the 39 (store_id, id) keys stay: about 1 microsecond and 7 bytes a row, measured.
- 341 loops hold a database call. Those over a parent's few rows follow use (one insert per order line or fee), within 3 statements per added line.
- The picture-use check still text-searches a site's documents for each picture, but only after something in the site was saved. It is on the slow list with a ceiling of 60 s a day; past that, uses are kept in a table.
- Reports and exports may average over 5 ms. They join the slow list as one family, with a ceiling, when owners start to use them.
- Search by meaning scans one store's vectors with no vector index, and the docs index whole, until a store passes about 10,000 products.
- A quiet instance closes its connections after 5 s and reconnects on the next request. That is the written design, so an instance is never frozen with connections open.
- Ending a sandbox runs 800 deletes and holds about 2,100 locks, one purge at a time: under 1 s for each ended sandbox.
- The leftover commerce_legacy schema (34 tables, 96 rows) and the unused columns cost nothing that runs. They go on the owner's word and when their tables are next changed.
