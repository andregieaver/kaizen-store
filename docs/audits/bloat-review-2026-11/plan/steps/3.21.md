# Step 3.21: The invoice job reads what is marked as waiting, and keeps today's full check as an hourly net

Phase 3 (One of each) of `docs/audits/bloat-review-2026-11.md`. Effort S. Read [how to carry out a step](../README.md) first.

**Who:** a smaller model; a stronger one reads the diff before the push. Every change is named, but it changes when legal documents (invoices and credit notes) are issued: a fast path that misses a case, or a credit-note call dropped where the SQL function did not run, passes the listed tests and leaves an order without its invoice until the hourly net, so a reviewer reads the invoice-issue.ts diff against the do_not list. *(Rating checked twice, 12 November; first written as strong.)*

**Goal:** The invoice job of the five-minute dispatcher reads only what is marked as waiting (a failed invoice, an applied order edit whose documents wait), through step 3.6's indexes, and today's full check of every store's 120 days runs once an hour as the net: an invoice tick, an email check and an e-invoice check read fewer than 100 rows of orders and of invoices in a store with 2,000 invoiced orders.

**Why:** The invoice job re-checks every store's orders of 120 days, every invoice and every refund on each five-minute tick (all 2,000 rows of each in the test store), its refunds branch has no time bound and no order before its limit, and the credit-note function runs twice per store. After the step a tick reads under 100 rows of each, the full check runs hourly, the credit notes are issued once per store, and saving the seller's details issues the waiting invoices at once instead of on the next tick.

**Closes:** [DQ-15](../../findings/queries/DQ-15.md). Split out of step 3.6 on 12 November. Each finding's file has the evidence.
