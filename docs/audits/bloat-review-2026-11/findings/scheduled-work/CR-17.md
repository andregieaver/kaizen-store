# CR-17 The daily job runs about 40 functions in one request under one ledger row, so a prune that fails is not seen

Scheduled and background work (`README.md` beside this file), part of `docs/audits/bloat-review-2026-11.md`.

- **Kind:** no check. **Priority:** P2. **Effort:** M. **Confidence:** medium. **Step:** 3.3.
- **Second check:** added by the planner, not checked a second time.
- **Where:** `src/app/api/cron/subscription-reminders/route.ts:61`, `src/app/api/cron/subscription-reminders/route.ts:63-78`, `src/app/api/cron/subscription-reminders/route.ts:79`, `src/app/api/cron/subscription-reminders/route.ts:88`, `src/server/analytics-visits.ts:313-342`, `src/server/ai-usage.ts:107-110`, `src/server/retention.ts:529-544`, `src/db/client.ts:68-70`, `docs/capacity.md:112`
- **Evidence:** Counted in the route: 13 functions start at once (line 61), 8 run one after another (lines 63-78), then 19 start at once (line 79): 40 in all, of which 24 are prunes. Two ledger rows cover them: route.subscription-reminders and daily.database-door. docs/capacity.md:112 asks that a prune 'never throws', and most obey by catching their own error: pruneVisits() logs a warning and returns what it managed (analytics-visits.ts:339-341); runRetention() keeps a failed step in its answer and the audit log (retention.ts:540-543); 19 calls in the route catch with console.error and answer 0 or an empty result (lines 69-79). So a prune that fails every day leaves a log line, and the ledger says the day went well. The first group has no catch at all: pruneUsage() is one unbatched delete with no try (ai-usage.ts:107-110), and if it or any of the other twelve throws, the request answers 500 and the retention schedule, the erasure resumes, the privacy reminders and the 19 prunes after it do not run that day. Both groups share five connections with a 5 s wait limit (db/client.ts:68-70), as in CR-2. pruneVisits() also loops over every store, closed ones included, with two deletes each, one after another.
- **Cost today:** No running cost today: it runs once a day. The exposure is the review's own subject: retention is the only brake on table growth (24 prunes) and part of the GDPR schedule (D162), and nothing shows per job whether it ran, failed or removed anything. cron.job_run_details (CR-5) is what a table looks like when nobody sees a missing prune. Paid by: The database later, in tables that grow unseen; the platform's admins, who hear of a failed day but not which job; owners and shoppers if an erasure or a retention step is skipped.
- **Saving:** No direct saving. A prune that stops working shows on the platform home within three days (FAILING_AFTER) instead of never, and one failing delete no longer skips the day's retention.
- **Risk:** A prune that used to fail quietly will now email the admins after three failed days; expect a few such emails at first, which is the point. The route test and the tax scan pin the file's text and fail CI until they follow.
- **Already tracked:** no (PSI REL-06 made the ledger for routes and dispatcher jobs; the daily route got one row)

## The fix

1. src/app/api/cron/subscription-reminders/route.ts: run the daily functions through runJobs('daily', 86_400, {...}) in CR-2's shape (small groups, one ledger write), so each has a row daily.<name> with its last run, its failure and, with CR-14, the rows it handled. One that throws no longer stops the others. Keep the route's own row and the JSON answer.
2. Remove the 19 '.catch((error) => (console.error(...), ...))' in the route (lines 69-79): runJobs() catches and logs through log() (D400). Let the prunes that swallow their own error report it: pruneVisits() (analytics-visits.ts:339-341) rethrows after logging what it managed; runRetention() throws at the end when run.errors is not empty, after writing its audit entry.
3. pruneVisits(): list only the stores that have rows older than the cutoff, or delete in batches across stores; keep the batches. If a new index on visits is wanted, build it concurrently in a file that starts with -- kaizen:no-transaction (visits is a hot table, D569).
4. Batch the unbatched deletes on tables that will be large (pruneUsage() on ai_usage is the first: delete where id in (select ... limit 5000), a few rounds), or run them in a transaction with longerTimeout().
5. src/server/cron-routes.scan.test.ts: every function the daily route calls is an entry of its runJobs() list.
6. docs/capacity.md:112: 'a prune that never throws' becomes 'a prune that is a job of the daily list: its failure is recorded and never stops the others'.

**Done when:** select count(*) from commerce.cron_jobs where job like 'daily.%' and every_seconds = 86400 is 38 or more, and a new case in cron-runs.int.test.ts shows a daily job that throws leaves the others' rows ok and its own counted as failed.
