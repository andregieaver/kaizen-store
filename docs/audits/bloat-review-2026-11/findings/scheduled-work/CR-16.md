Scheduled and background work (`README.md` beside this file), part of `docs/audits/bloat-review-2026-11.md`.

- **Kind:** eager. **Priority:** P2. **Effort:** M. **Confidence:** medium. **Step:** 3.23.
- **Second check:** added by the planner, not checked a second time.
- **Where:** `src/server/ledger.ts:1038-1071`, `src/server/ledger.ts:555-611`, `src/server/ledger.ts:488`, `src/server/ledger-compare.ts:45-69`, `src/server/tripletex-sync.ts:645-679`, `src/server/tripletex-sync.ts:515-523`, `src/server/tripletex-sync.ts:82-131`, `src/server/fiken-sync.ts:520-554`, `src/server/order-archive.ts:116-156`, `src/server/autopilot-settings.ts:63-75`, `src/server/autopilot-settings.ts:98-140`, `src/server/work-recurring.ts:694-731`
- **Evidence:** The verified report's clean list says only the loops of CR-3, CR-4, CR-6 and CR-9 grow. A scan of all 83 job functions for loops (scratch script, then each read) finds six more that do per-store work on every run. ledger: for every book that is open or closed in the last 60 days, one statement with ten 'union all' branches over invoices, credit notes, payments, refunds, payouts and disputes with two anti-joins (ledger.ts:555-611), then a read of the store's Tripletex connection (ledger-compare.ts:51); the books are taken 'order by updated_at' inside a 40 s budget, and the job never moves updated_at (only the owner's own edits do: ledger.ts:191 and 428, vat-return.ts:138), so the order is the same on every run. tripletex and fiken: for every connected store, the connection, two or more 'sweep' inserts, a claim and an 'attention' count, at least five statements, one store after another in 40 s (ordered by last_ok_at, which is fair). autoArchive: for every store with the option on, a candidates query over orders with four correlated subqueries, though its cutoff is whole days. autopilotBudgets: for every store with an AI budget, a sum over the month's ai_usage, for an email sent at most twice a month. recurring: for every store with an active repeating invoice, two reads, though periods are days. What is done well in the same files: a document that cannot be posted waits a day before the next try (ledger.ts:606-607), and Tripletex opens no session when a store has nothing to send.
- **Cost today:** Nothing for a store that does not use these features: the cost follows use, which is the right shape. For stores that do, it is paid per run and not per change. Estimate from the code: 1,000 stores with books and Tripletex are about 7,000 statements every five minutes, one after another inside 40 s budgets. Once the first books in the fixed order have real work that fills the 40 s, the books after them are not reached, run after run. Paid by: The database every five minutes, in proportion to the stores that use books, Tripletex, Fiken, auto-archive, an AI budget or repeating invoices; later, the owner whose book is last in the order.
- **Saving:** Estimate: none measurable today (few stores use these). At opening an idle book or connection costs no statement between hourly looks instead of 2 to 5 every five minutes, and no book can be starved by the order.
- **Risk:** A narrowing that misses a kind of document delays its posting or its voucher by up to an hour, never loses it (the hourly look takes it). It would be noticed as documents that reach the books or Tripletex on the hour. An invoice issued on its day by 'recurring' goes out up to an hour later than now.

## The fix

1. Ledger order (smallest, do first). Migration: commerce.ledger_books gains checked_at timestamptz. runLedgerPosting() (ledger.ts:1042-1046) orders by checked_at nulls first and sets checked_at = now() for each book after its turn, so every book gets a turn before any gets a second.
2. Skip an idle book cheaply: in the same first statement keep only books whose store has a document, payment, refund or payout newer than checked_at (five 'exists' on created_at or issued_at), or whose ledger_waiting rows are due their daily retry, or that were last checked over an hour ago. An idle book then costs nothing between hourly looks.
3. tripletex and fiken (tripletex-sync.ts:649-655, fiken-sync.ts:524-530): the same narrowing in the list statement: a store with an invoice or credit note issued since last_ok_at, a sync row not yet 'synced', or last looked at over an hour ago. Read docs/tripletex.md and docs/fiken.md first for any promised delay (the '40 seconds' in the dispatcher's comment is the run's budget, not a promise).
4. autoArchive, autopilotBudgets and recurring: hourly cadence in CR-2's list (their units are days and months).
5. Label all six 'per-store' in CR-14's registry with their cap and order.
