- **Evidence:** runJobs() starts 78 jobs at once with Promise.all and five more follow one after another, all with EVERY = 300. Nine are pure prunes with retention of 24 hours to 180 days, though docs/capacity.md:112 puts a prune in the daily cron. About 21 more are tied to a day, an hour or a week (the platform briefing at 07:00 Oslo, staff summaries, analytics alerts, report emails, autopilot reports and budgets, nightly translations, legal upkeep and sources, business changes, recurring invoices, auto-archive, Instagram, platform books, and houseCustomers, which rebuilds the list of every owner and applicant each run). Every job writes its own ledger row: 84 upserts a run, 565,049 so far on a table of about 100 rows. All of it shares a pool of 5 connections: the run takes 1.3 s while the jobs' recorded times sum to 12.1 s, so most recorded time is queueing. A query that waits more than 5 s for a connection throws (pg-pool). Added by the planner: the five trailing jobs and every revalidateTag (route.ts:262-279) wait for the slowest of the 78, and three of those carry budgets of 120 s (sms route.ts:195, legalWrites route.ts:246) and 40 s; so a page scheduled for 09:00 is published in the database at once but its cache is refreshed only when the slowest job ends.
- **Cost today:** At least 248 statements per idle run (the auditor's floor, not recounted), about 71,400 a day; small in database time. The real exposure is at opening: 83 jobs queue on 5 connections with a 5 s limit, so one job that holds connections for real work can make the others fail with a connection timeout. houseCustomers grows with every owner on the platform, every five minutes. Paid by: The database and the dispatcher's function, every five minutes; later, the jobs that time out behind a busy one.
- **Saving:** Estimate: about 8,100 job runs a day fewer from cadence (30 jobs x 288 become about 560), and ledger round trips from 84 to 1 per run (about 24,000 a day fewer). The larger gain is headroom: fewer jobs competing for five connections when real work arrives.
- **Risk:** An hourly job that fails is retried an hour later. A job slowed by mistake breaks a promise quietly (trials is the example found); the cadence list must be reviewed line by line. A store in a half-hour time zone gets an hourly job up to 30 minutes late (EU zones are whole hours). A ledger written at the end is lost if the function is killed mid-run; the job then reads as late on the platform home, which is the right signal. With a limiter, four slow jobs can hold the queue: that is why the budgeted jobs go last; if the run's own duration (route.cart-reminders in the ledger) passes 120 s, raise the limit or split the long jobs into a second runJobs.
- **Already tracked:** No. PSI REL-06 (done) made the ledger; the earlier advice to split the dispatcher was answered with maxDuration, not with cadences.

## The fix

1. src/lib/cron-health.ts: add a pure jobDue(every, lastStartedAt, now): every 300 is always due; 3,600 is due when lastStartedAt is null or before the start of the current UTC hour; 86,400 when it is before the start of the current UTC day. Unit-test it in src/lib/cron-health.test.ts.
2. src/server/cron-runs.ts runJobs(): take each job as { every: 300 | 3600 | 86400, run }. Read select job, last_started_at from commerce.cron_jobs where job like prefix || '.%' once at the start and run only the due jobs. An hourly job so runs in the first run after each full hour, as autopilot-effects.ts:44 and experiment-jobs.ts:50 already do by hand, and 'at seven in Oslo' still holds.
3. Same function: write the ledger once per run. Collect each job's start, end and error, then one multi-row insert ... on conflict (job) do update ... returning job, failures, last_ok_at; call alertFailingJob() for the rows that failed now and whose failures = FAILING_AFTER. Keep tracked() and record() for single jobs. Delete the RETIRED_JOBS rows once in a migration and stop deleting them on every run (cron-runs.ts:112-116); cronJobRows() filters retired names instead.
4. Same function: run at most 4 jobs at a time instead of 78 (a small limiter), so no statement waits 5 s for one of five connections. Order: the five-minute jobs with a promise first, the jobs with a time budget of their own last (sms 120 s, legalWrites 120 s, tripletex, fiken, ledger and pipedrive 40 s, platformBooks 30 s). Move the five trailing tracked() jobs (route.ts:266-275) into the same list and do each job's revalidateTag right after that job.
