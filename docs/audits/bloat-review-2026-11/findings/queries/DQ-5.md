- **Evidence:** runJobs() starts every job with Promise.all (cron-runs.ts:117); I counted 78 keys in the object the route passes, and 5 more tracked jobs run in turn after it. With the route's own that is 84 ledger upserts a tick (production: 457,349 + 107,700 calls, 84 s). The pool holds 5 connections and a caller waits at most 5 s for one (client.ts:68-70). Every job has the same cadence, 300 s, whether it prunes a log, settles trials or sends a cart reminder; the only cadences inside are hand-made (a minute-below-5 test at autopilot-effects.ts:44, claimDay() for one nightly job). Jobs that redo work on every tick whatever changed: embeddings computes an md5 over title, terms and description of every active product translation of every store on the platform's AI (embeddings.ts:31-45; 10,201 calls, 148 s) and calls commerce.store_is_active() per row, a SQL function with a SET clause, which Postgres cannot inline (69 uses in 44 files; I measured 2.4 µs a call against 0.05 µs for the plain expression); product saves already refresh their store's vectors. The invoice job, the knowledge job and the media job are DQ-15, DQ-6 and DQ-3.
- **Cost today:** Today small: the ledger writes are 0.15 ms each and the dispatcher's own run was 1.3 s. The jobs' recorded times sum to about 12.5 s because 78 jobs queue for 5 connections. What grows with stores: the embeddings hash (rows = all stores' active products times languages, every five minutes), the per-site loops, and the wait for a connection. When a waiter passes 5 s the job fails; that is a risk, not something the statistics show yet. Paid by: The database and one function every five minutes. Later: owners, when a job that serves them fails on a connect timeout.
- **Saving:** Estimate: most of the 148 s of the embeddings hash at today's size, and about a quarter of the 84 ledger writes a tick (the 20 or so jobs that become hourly or daily). The real gain is that the tick's work stops growing with every store's catalogue and that a slow job no longer starves the other 77 of connections.
- **Risk:** A job with too long a cadence or a wrong due test runs late: cron_jobs records every run with its own cadence, so lateness shows on the platform home and /status. Four at a time makes one tick longer; the route has maxDuration 300 and today's tick takes 1.3 s. A product changed outside the save path (an import) would wait for the nightly embeddings check: keep the import's own refresh call.
- **Already tracked:** partly: the PSI audit of 1 November 2026 lists the Promise.all against the pool of 5 as PERF-03 (unchanged, 'Cap the dispatcher's concurrency at the pool size'); docs/audits/psi-followups.md has no item for it. REL-06 (done) built the ledger and runJobs and set no cadence.

## The fix

1. src/server/cron-runs.ts runJobs(): take each job as { run, every } (every in seconds, 300 when left out) and run at most 4 at a time (the pool holds 5 and the ledger write needs one) through a small promise pool. At the start read the ledger once: select job, last_started_at from commerce.cron_jobs where job like the prefix. A job is due when it has no row or last_started_at is older than its every minus 60 s. Run only the due ones, each through tracked(name, every, run), so src/lib/cron-health.ts judges lateness by the job's own cadence (allowanceSeconds already scales). A job that is not due is not recorded and its row stays.
2. src/app/api/cron/cart-reminders/route.ts: give each of the 78 jobs its cadence with a one-line reason. Daily (86,400): the prunes (searches, apiIdempotency, oauthTokens, cache, docsCounts, chat, forms, recommendations, replications), the full embeddings check, the theme-shot sweep of DQ-1. Hourly (3,600): trials, autoArchive, stripeWebhooks, houseCustomers, legalUpkeep, legalSources, autopilotEffects (then remove its own minute test), media (DQ-3), the cookie scans' queueing (DQ-1), the document nets (DQ-15). Five minutes: whatever a shopper or owner waits on (carts, bookings, deliveries, schedules, tracking, summaries, status, the expiry jobs, payments, sms, supplierOrders, the invoice fast path, knowledge after DQ-6).
3. Embeddings, src/server/embeddings.ts:31-53 and 110-122: keep the refresh on product save (products/actions.ts:118 and the four other callers); the dispatcher's full hash check becomes daily; in its statement join commerce.stores s and write s.status = 'active' and not s.starter and not s.sandbox instead of calling commerce.store_is_active(t.store_id) per row.
4. Not to do: one batched ledger write. It saves about 12 ms of database time a tick and would lose every job's record when the function dies mid-run, which is what the ledger exists to show.
5. Update the wording of the rule in CLAUDE.md (Conventions: 'the five-minute dispatcher runs each job through runJobs()') to say each job names its cadence, and docs/tripletex.md:33 ('runs its jobs side by side') to say four at a time.
