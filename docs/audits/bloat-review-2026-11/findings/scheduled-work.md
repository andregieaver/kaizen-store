# Scheduled and background work (CR)

Part of `docs/audits/bloat-review-2026-11.md`, on commit 0bd814c. One reviewer's reading of this area. A tick (✓) marks a finding the lead checked a second time; the others were not checked. "Step" is the step of the plan that closes the finding.

**In short.** Scheduled work is the platform's steadiest load, and almost all of it finds nothing to do. Supabase wakes a 47 to 125 MB Vercel function 13,250 times a day, and those runs send roughly 170,000 statements a day to the database; the call that wakes the cron routes is the most expensive statement the app's role runs (3,974 s in total). Two of the nine every-minute routes and 36 of the 83 five-minute jobs serve features whose tables have never held a row, and 31 jobs run every five minutes for work that is daily or slower. The parts are carefully built (claims, leases, a run ledger, alerts), so the bloat is in when things run, not how: one gated tick, a cadence per job and a handful of small fixes remove most of it.

## Well made, and should stay

- Claims and leases are sound: every queue is claimed with 'for update skip locked' and an expiry, so overlapping runs never do a piece of work twice (e.g. src/server/broadcasts.ts:465-482, src/server/integrations.ts:1031-1041, src/server/dream-factory.ts:463-472).
- The run ledger and its alerts (src/server/cron-runs.ts, src/lib/cron-health.ts) are worth keeping: a late or failing job shows on the platform home and one failing job does not stop the others. Only how often it is written needs changing.
- Chromium is imported lazily (src/server/browser.ts:11,15) and next.config.ts keeps it to four routes; an idle run of a Chromium route never starts or even loads the browser.
- Network calls are mostly gated by a database check first: Tripletex and Fiken sync (tripletex-sync.ts:515-523), the ledger comparison (ledger-compare.ts:51-69), stores' payout reads (once a day per store, five stores a run, payouts.ts:184-189), parcel tracking caps.
- Request-side after() is used well: 32 files outside cron kick work the request itself just created (page jobs in site-tools.ts:245,322,367; a broadcast in broadcasts/actions.ts:93; gift cards in shopper-emails.ts:422; inbound email in support-inbound.ts:148; embeddings after a product save in products/actions.ts:118). This is why most every-minute polls are only a safety net. The store admin layout adds two after() checks per full page load (src/app/admin/(gated)/[store]/layout.tsx:68-73); each is one cheap query when nothing is missing, so I left it out of the findings.
- status_checks is not unpruned, as the brief assumed: src/server/status.ts:266 deletes checks older than 91 days. The table shows 0 deletes because it is only about 14 days old.
- net._http_response prunes itself: pg_net keeps six hours. docs/decisions.md D263 counted 792 kept calls on 16 October, exactly six hours of the three jobs scheduled then, and the repo changes no setting.
- The daily route (src/app/api/cron/subscription-reminders/route.ts) carries about 30 retention prunes, so most growing tables do have a rule, as docs/capacity.md:112 asks.
- Cron auth is cheap and safe: the Vault secret is read at most every five minutes per instance and compared in constant time (src/server/cron-auth.ts:18-37).
- The cadence patterns already exist inside the dispatcher: two jobs limit themselves to once an hour (autopilot-effects.ts:44, experiment-jobs.ts:50) and the nightly translation claims its day (cron-runs.ts:81-91). The fix for the other jobs is to use them, not to invent something.

## Findings

### CR-1 ✓ Nine routes are woken every minute whether or not anything waits

- **Kind:** eager. **Effort:** M. **Confidence:** high. **Step:** 0.3.
- **Where:** `supabase/migrations/20261111172842_baseline_4_roles_and_supabase.sql:69-103`, `src/app/api/cron/autopilot/route.ts:18-33`, `src/app/api/cron/broadcasts/route.ts:24-46`, `src/app/api/cron/integrations/route.ts:24-41`, `src/app/api/cron/page-jobs/route.ts:16-25`, `src/app/api/cron/pack-pictures/route.ts:16-28`, `src/app/api/cron/page-looks/route.ts:19-37`, `src/app/api/cron/dream-factory/route.ts:17-34`, `src/server/autopilot-runner.ts:55-75`, `src/server/autopilot-events.ts:83-110`, `src/server/integrations.ts:1031-1062`, `src/db/client.ts:13-14`, `src/db/client.ts:68-77`, `next.config.ts:51-58`, `docs/capacity.md:200-203`
- **Evidence:** pg_cron posts to nine routes every minute: 12,960 calls a day. cron_jobs says they last took 0 to 72 ms, and seven of them answer before working, so they found nothing. Reading each idle path: autopilot 13 queries, broadcasts 15, integrations 14, theme-shots 8, cookie-scan 3, dream-factory 2, the other three 1 each: 58 queries plus 10 ledger upserts a minute. integrations runs an update and a delete on integration_deliveries every minute (66,533 calls each) though the table never held a row. Each call loads a function of 47 to 125 MB (measured from the build's .nft.json); page-jobs requires 118 chunks, 10.9 MB of JavaScript, to run one claim query. Three of the nine carry Chromium (125.4 MB each). Most queues are already kicked by the request that fills them (site-tools.ts:245, broadcasts/actions.ts:93, support-inbound.ts:148, triggerLook in page-looks.ts:126-139), and every queue table already has a partial index on its waiting rows (e.g. page_ai_jobs_waiting_idx, integration_deliveries_due_idx, theme_shots_queue_idx, dream_runs_due_idx, store_events_due_idx).
- **Cost today:** 12,960 function starts a day (about 394,000 a month) and about 97,900 statements a day for nothing. In the database, net.http_post is the most expensive statement of the app's role: 352,514 calls, 3,974 s. Estimate: each call also keeps an instance awake at least 5 s after its last query (attachDatabasePool with a 5 s idle timeout), so nine routes hold about 45 instance-seconds every minute. Paid by: The server per invocation (Vercel), the database, and the Supabase pooler (a fresh pool per call).
- **Fix:** Keep the routes; change the trigger. Replace the nine every-minute schedules with one pg_cron job that runs a small SQL function each minute: for each route one cheap exists() on its queue, and net.http_post only when it is true; plus every route once every 15 minutes as a safety net for time-derived work. Gates: page-jobs = page_ai_jobs waiting; page-looks = page_looks queued; pack-pictures = store_starters.pictures_wanted_by set; dream-factory = dream_runs unfinished; theme-shots = theme_shots waiting or running; cookie-scan = cookie_scans queued; broadcasts = a broadcast scheduled or sending, or any automation, review setting, due gift card or ad connection on; autopilot = any autopilot_settings row, pending plan step or active goal; integrations = store_events due, deliveries pending, workflow runs pending, inbound emails pending, or an open support conversation. Raise the routes' everySeconds from 60 to 900 so cron-health does not call a skipped route late, and have the tick keep one ledger row of its own. First step (S): add 'where exists(...) or minute % 15 = 0' to each existing schedule's command in one migration. This keeps the rule in docs/capacity.md:202 (heavy work has a route of its own): the reason there is Chromium and time limits, and the routes stay apart.
- **Saving:** Estimate: nearly all of the 12,960 daily calls and about 97,900 statements a day while the features are idle as today; with only the 15-minute safety net firing, 864 calls a day (minus 93%). pg_cron runs fall from 13,250 to about 1,730 a day. When a store really uses a feature, its route runs again each minute, so cost follows use.
- **Risk:** A gate that forgets one kind of due work delays it to the 15-minute sweep; it is never lost. Promised timings read: the autopilot reacts to a waiting customer after 15 minutes by default (autopilot-events.ts:76), so its gate must stay true while any store has it on; automations, review requests and gift cards count in days; workflow schedules in hours. Add a scan test: every cron route names its gate.
- **Already tracked:** Partly. The PSI audits advise 'Fold the every-minute routes into one' as PERF-10 (docs/audits/psi-2026-10-24.md:146, repeated on 28 Oct, 31 Oct and 1 Nov) but it never became an item in psi-followups.md and is not done. The open item REL-06 (psi-followups.md:477) would add a ledger write to seven of these routes each minute; do it inside this change so it is written only when work ran.

### CR-2 ✓ The dispatcher runs all 83 jobs every five minutes: one cadence for everything, one ledger write per job

- **Kind:** eager. **Effort:** M. **Confidence:** high. **Step:** 0.4.
- **Where:** `src/app/api/cron/cart-reminders/route.ts:92`, `src/app/api/cron/cart-reminders/route.ts:127-275`, `src/server/cron-runs.ts:23-56`, `src/server/cron-runs.ts:106-119`, `src/server/search.ts:428-439`, `src/server/api-idempotency.ts:73-81`, `src/server/oauth.ts:821-835`, `src/server/house-customers.ts:169-256`, `src/server/platform-briefing.ts:210-216`, `src/server/legal-sources.ts:195-228`, `src/server/autopilot-effects.ts:44`, `src/lib/cron-health.ts:26-28`, `docs/capacity.md:112`
- **Evidence:** runJobs() starts 78 jobs at once and five more follow, all with EVERY = 300. Nine are pure prunes with retention of 24 hours to 180 days (searches 90 days, search cache 30, forms 30, docs reads 90, idempotency keys 24 hours, OAuth tokens, chat counts, recommendation events, page copies), although docs/capacity.md:112 says a prune belongs in the daily cron. About 22 more are tied to a day, an hour or a week: the platform briefing (07:00 Oslo), staff summaries, analytics alerts (after 20:00), report emails, autopilot reports and budgets, nightly translations, legal upkeep (daily per store), legal sources (weekly per source, checked 2,016 times a week), trials, business changes, recurring invoices, bonus birthdays, auto-archive, Instagram (hourly), platform books, and houseCustomers, which reconciles every account and request in one transaction of at least 14 statements each run. Every job also writes its own ledger row: 84 upserts a run, 565,049 upserts so far on a table of about 100 rows.
- **Cost today:** At least 248 statements per idle run, about 71,400 a day, through a pool of 5 connections. The jobs queue for those connections: the run takes 1.3 s but the jobs' recorded times add up to 12.5 s. Paid by: The database and the dispatcher's function, every five minutes, whatever the store count.
- **Fix:** Give runJobs() a cadence per job: every 300, 3,600 or 86,400 seconds, decided from one read of cron_jobs at the start (last_started_at is already there). Move the nine prunes to the daily route. Run the day-and-hour jobs hourly (the two jobs that already test getUTCMinutes() show the pattern); keep at five minutes only what promises minutes: status, page schedules, cart and SMS reminders, support idle and summaries, follow-ups, Life hand-offs, activity pushes, live drills, the continuations (data jobs, store copies, shop moves, Pipedrive, legal writes), expiries, tracking, payments, invoices and the accounting syncs. Write the ledger once per run: one multi-row upsert at the end, and at once only for a failure.
- **Saving:** Estimate: job runs fall from 23,904 to about 15,500 a day from cadence alone (31 jobs x 288 become about 540), and ledger writes from 84 to 1 per run (minus 24,000 round trips a day). Together with CR-3, statements fall from about 71,400 to about 25,000 a day.
- **Risk:** An hourly job that fails is retried an hour later, not five minutes later. cron-health already scales its allowance with every_seconds, so 'late' still works. A ledger written at the end is lost when the function is killed mid-run; the job then reads as late, which is the truth.
- **Already tracked:** No. PSI REL-06 (done) made the ledger; the earlier advice to split the dispatcher (psi-2026-10-24.md:158) was answered with maxDuration, not with cadences.

- **Risk:** A missed 'mark stale' leaves a vector or the chat's knowledge out of date until the hourly sweep. Search by meaning and the chat agent are affected, so keep the sweep.
- **Already tracked:** no (psi-2026-10-24.md:146 judged these 'incremental'; the stats show the knowledge loop and the media re-touch are not)

### CR-8 Kaizena Flow's own payouts are read from Stripe every five minutes

- **Kind:** eager. **Effort:** S. **Confidence:** high. **Step:** 0.4.
- **Where:** `src/server/platform-books.ts:238-245`, `src/server/platform-payouts.ts:80-107`, `src/server/stripe.ts:58-60`, `src/server/payouts.ts:184-189`, `src/server/invoice-issue.ts:119`, `src/server/subscriptions.ts:791-810`
