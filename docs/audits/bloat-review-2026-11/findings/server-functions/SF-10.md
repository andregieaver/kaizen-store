# SF-10 ✓ Nine full-application functions are called every minute, and before opening they find nothing to do

Server functions: size, cold start, per-request overhead (`README.md` beside this file), part of `docs/audits/bloat-review-2026-11.md`.

- **Kind:** eager. **Priority:** P3. **Effort:** M. **Confidence:** medium. **Step:** 3.1.
- **Second check:** corrected.
- **Where:** `supabase/migrations/20261111172842_baseline_4_roles_and_supabase.sql:93-104`, `src/app/api/cron/page-jobs/route.ts:7-25`, `next.config.ts:54-58`, `docs/rules/admin/ai.md:126`, `docs/rules/admin/website.md:129`, `docs/rules/admin/website.md:238`
- **Evidence:** Nine cron routes are scheduled every minute (read in the baseline migration, lines 93-104: autopilot, broadcasts, cookie-scan, dream-factory, integrations, pack-pictures, page-jobs, page-looks, theme-shots): 12,960 calls a day by the schedule. Each is its own entry. Re-measured traces: page-jobs 451 files and 47.3 MB; cookie-scan 615 files and 125.4 MB with Chromium. On the graph /api/cron/page-jobs reaches 1,308 files (502 after SF-1's six cuts). The every-minute rhythm is written into the rules (a look, a page job, a theme photograph starts at once from the press, else within a minute), so it is by design. Called every minute, these functions stay warm between deploys and start cold after each.
- **Cost today:** 12,960 invocations a day and up to nine warm instances of about 80 MB, three with a 125 MB package. Today that is idle cost. Once stores use these features the queues hold work most minutes and the calls are no longer idle: this cost follows use by itself as the platform fills. Paid by: The server (invocations, memory); the database (one http_post and one ledger upsert per run).
- **Saving:** From the schedule: about 12,960 heavy calls a day become 1,440 light ones while the queues are empty. The saving shrinks as stores arrive and the queues fill, so it is worth least when the platform is busiest.
- **Risk:** A queue check that misses one kind of due work delays it, hence CR-1's sweep. Nothing in this entry adds risk of its own.
- **Already tracked:** no existing plan; inside this review it is CR-1 (scheduled work) and DP-7 (the Chromium routes).

## The fix

1. Do not build a router here. The scheduled-work plan of this review owns the change: CR-1 (one every-minute tick that asks the database which queues hold due work and calls only those routes, with a 15-minute sweep as the safety net) and DP-7 (the due check for the three Chromium routes).
2. The functions' share is SF-1: its steps 1 to 5 make each of the six non-Chromium routes lighter whatever the queues hold. src/app/api/cron/page-jobs/route.ts is in the reach baseline of SF-7 for that reason.
3. When CR-1's tick route is written, hold it in src/server/route-load.graph.test.ts as a light entry: it needs the database client, cron-auth and the log, nothing of the nine routes' own code.

**Done when:** CR-1's own check holds (in production, select count(*) from cron.job where schedule = '* * * * *' returns 1; 9 today), and the tick route is in src/server/route-load.baseline.json at 120 files or fewer.

**Tests and rules that hold today's behaviour, and change with it:**

- src/server/cron-routes.scan.test.ts (every cron route has a maxDuration and runs inside trackedRoute()): the tick route must satisfy it.
- docs/rules/admin/ai.md:126 and docs/rules/admin/website.md:129, :238: 'starts within a minute' stays true under CR-1's design.

**Comes after:** CR-1 of the scheduled-work plan (owns the scheduler change); SF-1 steps 1 to 5

**Notes:** P3 here because the work is planned once, under CR-1, and because the idle calls are what one expects before opening: with stores using these features the calls do work. The skeptic made the same correction.
