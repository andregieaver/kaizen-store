# D534: Layers over a row's or column's background, ready-made backdrops, and one hue slider for the whole

**Layers over a row's or column's background, ready-made backdrops, and one hue slider for the whole (10 November 2026, `src/lib/background-layers.ts`, `background-layers-schema.ts`, `src/lib/backdrops.ts`, `src/lib/hue.ts`, `BackgroundLayers` in `src/components/page-parts.tsx`, `layerFx()` in `src/lib/motion-attrs.ts`, `src/app/motion.css`, `src/components/admin/backdrop-fields.tsx`).** A row or column keeps its background as it was (a colour, picture, gradient, video or none) and takes up to
six **layers** over it, under what it holds (`backgroundLayers`, the lowest first): a **glow** (one soft ellipse, a colour at its heart and optionally another around it, placed and sized in per cent of the part), a **pattern** (grid, dots or stars in one colour, a size in pixels, fading towards the edges, the bottom or the top) and a **horizon** (a disc far wider than the part whose lit top edge shows as an arc; flat, gentle or round). **Real elements, not pseudo-elements:** the layers are
children of a frame of their own (`[data-fx-layers]`, `overflow: hidden`, behind the content as a background's frame is), because a part has only two pseudo-elements, Spotlight and Shine already use `::after`, an owner's own CSS may use both, and neither can be switched off or moved one by one. **Each moves by itself:** the frame names the scroll timeline a background's own layer already follows (`--fx-bg`), and each layer plays one keyframe along it with its own travel (`scroll`, -100 far to
100 close, as `--fx-sk`, measured in the frame's height), so layers of one part move apart; a pattern that travels is taller than its frame by its travel. By itself a layer can *twinkle* or *breathe*.
Only `translate`, `scale` and `opacity` change; a glow and a horizon are gradients on a box no larger than the frame needs (a quarter taller, for the travel), never a blur filter, a shadow or an element many screens large, so however large one is asked to be the browser keeps a surface of about the row's size; "reduce motion" draws everything still; browsers without CSS scroll timelines get it from the motion runtime (`motionNeeds()` counts a travelling layer). **Nothing an owner types becomes
CSS:** a layer is names from lists, `#rrggbb` colours and whole numbers inside limits (`backgroundLayersSchema`), written as `data-fx-*` attributes and `--fx-*` custom properties remade by `layerDraw()`. **The hue** (`backgroundHue`, -180 to 180): one slider turns every colour of the background and its layers together (a colour base in the part stylesheet, a gradient's colours, a colour over a picture, each layer), in Oklab so a colour stays as light and as strong as it was and a grey stays a
grey (`shiftHue()`); a dark backdrop stays dark at every turn, which a test holds for each ready-made one. *Match my theme* turns it to the theme's accent where that has a hue. **Ready-made backdrops** (`BACKDROPS`: Horizon, Grid, Starfield, Soft dots, Sunrise): plain page content put on a part in one press (its background, layers, no hue; what moved or blurred the old background is taken away), then the owner's to change. **Editing:** Homer gets the backdrops as small pictures right under the
background's kind, then the hue slider with a strip showing what each place gives; Lisa gets *Customise the layers* one press away: the list (top first), each layer shown or hidden, moved, removed, and its settings (colours, place, size, fade, opacity, travel, movement by itself).
The panel is fetched when a background's settings are first opened and its English is in `backdrops.ts` (a server-only area `backdrops` handed down in Norwegian, as `storeParts`), so no other screen's first scripts grow. **Text over it:** `surfaceOf()` reads the background in its turned colours, so the row is still marked `kz-on-dark` or `kz-on-light` (D390).
The page check now reads what the site draws: text with no colour of its own on a part whose background is light or dark is held to the theme's colour made for that background (`ThemeSet.onLight`/`onDark` from `themeSetsOf()`), whatever set the page shows; before, a dark row with theme text was reported as unreadable *in the light colours* although the site drew it light, which every dark backdrop would have met at Publish.
The layers themselves are not measured (where they lie under the text is not known to the check): a strong glow right behind text is for the eye. **The platform:** Kaizena Flow's own pages use the same builder and renderer, so it has it in the same change. **Not in this step:** a picture as a layer, saved backdrops (D183), a theme's section style carrying one (D242), and the AI manager's design tools and *Make my page cool* choosing or tuning layers; they are the next step.
No migration (pages are JSON checked by zod; a row without layers is stored as before).
