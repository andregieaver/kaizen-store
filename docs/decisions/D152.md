# D152: A store analytics cockpit with drill-down

**A store analytics cockpit with drill-down.** Under Analytics in the store admin: an Overview of twelve figures (revenue, net revenue, orders, conversion, average order, gross profit and margin, new and returning customers, refund rate, sessions, revenue per visitor) against the previous period and last year, with sales and profit charts, the funnel, best sellers, channels, a forecast, target progress, alerts and a plain-words explanation of why sales changed (traffic × conversion × basket,
and the segment that moved most); then Finance, Customers (cohorts, RFM, LTV, repeat rate), Products, Inventory, Marketing (channels, CAC, ROAS, discounts, spend entry), Subscriptions, Traffic (devices, countries, searches, refunds, weekday and hour) and Settings. Every figure has one definition in `docs/analytics.md`, worked out in code.

Date: 2026-10-02

Asked for by the owner as the answer to four questions: are we making money, why are sales changing, which customers and products drive the business, what needs doing today. Costs are new data (`product_variants.cost_minor`, kept on the order line when sold) so profit is real, with payment fees, shipping costs and fixed costs as the owner's labelled estimates; profit always says how much of sales it covers and never shows zero for unknown.
Visit counting is a new, off-by-default store setting without cookies, storage, IP address or user agent (a visitor hash that changes every store day, a whitelisted landing path, tied to a cart made that day for channel attribution), so conversion, funnel, devices and channels exist without a consent banner; its wording is deliberately not 'anonymous'. This supersedes D104's 'visits and product views are not tracked' while counting is on.
Not tracked and said on the pages: refunds made only in Stripe's dashboard, return reasons beyond free text, subscription expansion and reactivation, regions inside a country, revenue after a search, anything before counting began. Reviewed adversarially by five lenses (25 defects confirmed and fixed, among them a blocker that stored secret link tokens in landing paths).
