# D479: The language a visitor sees first, the language switcher, and the currency with the language

**The language a visitor sees first, the language switcher, and the currency with the language (`src/lib/language-rule.ts`, `src/lib/language-view.ts`, `src/lib/language-switch.ts`, `LanguageLinks`, `languageSwitchStyle()`).** Asked for by the owner: how a store with several languages decides which one a visitor sees, a language switcher an owner places and shapes, and a currency that can follow the language. **The rule** (`stores.language_rule`, Settings → Languages and currencies, *The
language a visitor sees first*, while Several languages is on and the store has more than one): `browser` (`Accept-Language`; `no` and `nn` read Bokmål), `location-browser` (the browser's language where the store has one it asks for, else where they are), `location` (the language of the country the visitor is in, `x-vercel-ip-country`, `COUNTRY_LANGUAGES`) and `own` (each country's own language). **The default** (the owner, 6 November 2026): a store made from now on starts with `browser`; every
store made before keeps `own`, as it was, so no live store changes (the migration adds the column with `own` and then makes `browser` its default). A store made from a template does not take the template's rule; a store's copy (D129, `duplicate_store()`) keeps its original's three settings.
Under every rule the visitor's own choice comes first: choosing a language in a switcher (the new one or the country menu's) keeps it in `kaizen_lang_{storeId}` for a year, a cookie the store's cookie page declares as necessary (the visitor's own choice of how the store is shown, as light or dark, D99), set only while Several languages is on. **Only the front door decides:** a store in one country sends a visitor at `/` (or `/s/{store}`) on its own address to their language's address (`/en`,
`/en-eur`) with a 307 that is private and never cached (`languageAtDoor()`, the proxy); never a crawler, never on a country's or language's own domain, never any other address.
A store in several countries keeps its chooser (D338: it suggests, never redirects) and its links lead each country in the language the rule picks there (`firstSlug()`). *Why only the front door:* a link to a page in one language (a search result, an advertisement, a shared link) is the version the visitor asked for, and the Geo-blocking Regulation (EU 2018/302, art. 3(2)) forbids sending a visitor to a version in another language by where they are without their consent; search engines also need
each language's addresses to stay put.
The settings page says so in a sentence. **The switcher** is the site part *Languages*, now a store's as well as Kaizena Flow's (`SITE_PARTS.languages`, shown while the visitor has more than one language): side by side (the default), one under another or a drop-down list; each language by its name in itself (the default), its short code (`EN`) or no words; with a flag or not (the country its variant names, `flagOf()`: `en-IE` Irish, else where most of its readers are; 33 flags from flag-icons
7.2.3, MIT, in `public/flags/`, Spain's and Croatia's about 16 kB gzipped). Each of the three can differ by screen size (`SiteBlock.switcher`, `langText`, `langFlag` and the same keys in `at`, D179): the page is drawn once with the list and the menu, names, codes and flags, and the part stylesheet shows what each size asks (`languageSwitchStyle()`; a name not shown is still read, so every link keeps its name; no words and no flag is the short code).
The builder edits each at the size chosen, with the device mark, and its stand-in draws the look at that size.
A store's switcher goes to the page the shopper is on in that language (as `ViewMenu`, so pages stay prerendered). **The currency with the language** (`stores.currency_follows_language`, `stores.language_currencies`, while Several languages and Several currencies are both on): choosing a language also chooses its currency: the owner's choice per language, else the currency of the country its variant names (`regionCurrency()`: `en-IE` euro, `en-GB` pounds, `sv-SE` kronor), where the country can
be shown in it (`offers()`), else the country's own; the country menu's languages, the switcher, the front door and the chooser all follow it (`languageSlug()`). The currency menu still chooses on its own. **The platform:** Kaizena Flow's own *Languages* part takes the same look settings, by size (`LanguageSwitch` with the part's class; its standard footer and phone menu stay as they were).
Kaizena Flow's site keeps D312's rule, suggesting and never sending a visitor to a language; the same choice of rule for it (a `platform_settings` column read by the proxy at `/`) is the next step, listed in `docs/platform-parity.md`; it sells plans, whose currency is not a page's, so the currency part does not apply there.
