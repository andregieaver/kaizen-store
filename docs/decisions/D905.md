# D905: Rivals for every site: the market check without a shop (market check M5)

Date: 2026-11-26

**Rivals for every site (26 November 2026, the owner: M2 to M4 require the online store, "and offer no value to sites with it turned off.
How can we bring value to these as well in terms of competitor watch and analysis?", then "write the spec first, then build step one").**
The spec is [`docs/market-check.md`](../market-check.md) 7.

- **What every site has.** A site without a shop has no products or prices to hold against a rival's offer, but every site can be held
  against what its rivals publish for anyone and robots to read: the pages they add and take down (sitemaps), where they stand in search,
  their speed for their visitors, the prices they publish for services, and the store's own enquiries per country. Facts by code with
  their source, findings by code, words by the model, as D900.
- **In five steps.** M5a, built here: rivals for every store, and what changed on their sites from their own sitemaps. M5b: search
  visibility for up to 10 words a market, monthly. M5c: speed from the Chrome UX Report. M5d: service prices kept by hand with their page,
  checked there by code (M4's way). M5e: enquiries as the conversion of a store without orders.
- **A sitemap, never a site's words.** Only the addresses a site lists for robots, robots.txt first and on its own host, each file counted
  in the store's pages for the day and kept as a source; no page is fetched. Reading a site's words in bulk would meet the EU database
  right (Directive 96/9/EC); a list of addresses is what the site offers robots.
- **Never kept or read.** Google ratings over time (the Places terms: shown when fetched, never kept), social media pages, anything behind
  a sign-in.
- **What needs products stays the shop's.** A rival is any store's; reading its product pages, the offers and the price findings need the
  shop, as before.
- **Homer and Lisa.** Homer meets a rival's growth only as one of the three findings, with *Make a plan*; each rival's site (pages, added,
  gone, the newest) is in *Your rivals*, Lisa's part.
- **The platform.** Kaizena Flow's own store gets rivals in the same change: other platforms' sites; what it published counts its own
  pages and the platform site's.

- **Service prices, as built (M5d).** A rival's published price for a service kept by a person with its page, checked there by code when
  kept and monthly (`priceOnPage()`), held against the store's own as the owner gives it or a product's; only a price found on its page
  makes a finding (`service_cheaper`, `service_dearer`). Kaizena Flow's own are its plans, held against other platforms' on Platform →
  Plans (M4).

- **Search visibility, as built (M5b).** Up to 10 words a country followed by a person, searched monthly by code through the platform's
  search (only the word and the country sent, counted in the store's searches), the store's and its rivals' first places on the page
  kept with the store's place before; a rival above is a weakness, the store near the top with none above a strength.

- **Speed, as built (M5c).** The Chrome UX Report (free) read monthly for each store's site and its rivals' with the platform's key,
  set on Platform → AI; Google's thresholds in code; a rival good where the store is poor a weakness.

Idle cost: none of AI. A rival's sitemap is read weekly by the five-minute dispatcher, one to six files, and the paths kept are at most
1,000 a rival. Service prices: two pages a run where prices are due, a table of a few rows a rival. Search: two searches a run where words are due, at
most ten words a country.
