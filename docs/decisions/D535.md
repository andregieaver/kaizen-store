# D535: A layer over a background can be blurred, for depth

**A layer over a background can be blurred, for depth (10 November 2026, `blur` on a `BackgroundLayer`, `LAYER_BLUR_MAX`, `layerDraw()` in `src/lib/background-layers.ts`, `[data-fx-soft]` in `src/app/motion.css`, the *Blur* slider in `src/components/admin/backdrop-fields.tsx`).** Every layer of D534 (a glow, a pattern, a horizon) takes `blur`, whole pixels from 1 to 40, sharp when left out: out of focus, as what is far away or very close is, which together with its travel on scroll is what sets
one layer in front of another. **Painted once, then only moved:** a layer is now two elements, the one that travels (`[data-fx-bglayer]`, which carries the scroll and the movement by itself) and inside it the one that draws (the glow, the disc, the pattern). The blur is a `filter` on the element that draws, never on the one that travels, so the browser paints the blurred picture once and the scroll only moves it; a filter on the travelling element would be worked out again on every frame.
Nothing animates the blur.
What draws reaches past the frame by twice the blur on every side (`--fx-e`), as a blurred background picture does, so its soft rim never shows at the part's edges. **As page content:** `blur` is a whole number inside its limit (`backgroundLayersSchema`) and reaches the page as `data-fx-soft` and `--fx-blur` from `layerDraw()`; nothing an owner types becomes CSS. **Editing:** a *Blur* slider in every layer's settings, after *Opacity*, with a line saying what it is for. **Ready-made:**
Starfield's near stars are 2 px out of focus over its sharp far ones; the other backdrops are as they were. This takes back one sentence of D534, *never a blur filter*: that was about drawing a glow with one where a gradient does; a layer's own blur is the one filter, and where it is drawn keeps it cheap. **The platform:** the same builder and renderer, so Kaizena Flow's own pages have it in the same change. No migration.
