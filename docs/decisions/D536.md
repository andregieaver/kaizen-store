# D536: A video can play once and rest on its last frame, shows the page through a see-through background, and its upload is sent twice before it fails

**A video can play once and rest on its last frame, shows the page through a see-through background, and its upload is sent twice before it fails (10 November 2026, `src/lib/video-play.ts`, `src/lib/upload-retry.ts`, `VideoBlock.once`, `src/components/video-view.tsx`, `src/components/admin/video-upload.tsx`).** The owner put an animated logo, a WebM with a see-through background, in the Video component: the upload failed (*could not be uploaded.
Try again.*), and the component could only loop a video that starts by itself. **What production showed (10 November 2026):** Storage's logs have the signed upload made and the browser's pre-check answered, and no upload after either, while an MP4 from the same phone a minute earlier arrived; the bucket takes WebM and no rule of ours refused it. The request stopped in the phone's browser before the file left it.
The server cannot tell which of the two usual causes it was (the line dropping, or the browser giving up on a file the phone reported as changed after it was chosen), so the upload no longer depends on either: what is sent is first read into the tab's memory, and it is sent through `putWithRetry()`, a second time when the first try never got an answer or Storage itself failed, never again when Storage refused it, and a second try that finds the file already there counts as sent.
The media library had kept the video as its upload started (D88) and showed a video that did not exist; one that never arrives is now forgotten again (`StartVideo` called with `abandoned`, `forgetUnstoredVideo()`, which only forgets the owner's own row whose path holds no file). Taking a still can no longer hold an upload up for ever (8 seconds, then none). **Playing once:** how an uploaded video plays is one pure function, `videoPlay()`, read by the site's player and by the builder's fields.
A video that starts by itself looped, always; it now loops unless `once` is set. The builder's *Loop* switch, greyed out before while *Start by itself* was on, is the one control: off, it writes `once`, and the video plays once and rests on its last frame. A new key rather than a new meaning for `loop`: every video already set to start by itself has no `loop` and must go on looping.
Played once, a video shows no picture before it plays (its still is taken a little way in, and would flash before the first frame), and shows its last frame to visitors who prefer less motion and where the browser will not start it (a phone saving power), instead of nothing.
With the controls switched off, which a video that starts by itself could already do, that is an animated logo. **See-through:** the player drew a black box behind every uploaded video, which only showed while one without a picture loaded, and behind a video with a see-through background, where it defeated it. The box is gone.
`compressVideo()` re-encodes to H.264, which has no transparency, so it now leaves a video that can be transparent as it is (`canBeTransparent()`). **Sound:** the upload button was written for a row's background and dropped every video's sound when it compressed, and said *plays without sound, on a loop* under the Video component and the gallery too; those two now keep the sound (`keepSound`) and no longer show that hint.
The admin's English words ride in every admin screen's first scripts, where the home screen had about 240 bytes of room (`e2e/signed-in/admin-scripts.spec.ts`, D533), so this added none: the Loop switch is explained in *Start by itself*'s hint, shortened to make the room, and a video that plays once shows no picture field instead of words saying its picture is not used. **Not done, and said to the owner:** Safari ignores a WebM's transparency and draws it on black, on every iPhone.
An ordinary MP4 holding the picture above its own mask, drawn by a few lines of script, works in every browser and was tested in the HTML component's frame; the Video component playing such a file itself is the next step, not built here. A copied page's video that started by itself without looping plays once in the copy too (`replicate-build.ts`).
Kaizena Flow's own pages are built with the same builder and player and upload through `startPlatformVideoUploadAction`, so the platform has all of it. Tests: `video-play.test.ts`, `upload-retry.test.ts`, `video-view.test.ts`, `page-components.test.ts`, `replicate-build.test.ts`. No migration.
