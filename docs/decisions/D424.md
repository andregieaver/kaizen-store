# D424: Two pages side by side, in more ways

**Two pages side by side, in more ways: scroll, swap, close, resize, and one page on the computer and the phone at once.** Over the dual page (D412): *Scroll together* as a switch; *Swap sides*; each page's *Close* empties its half, *Close both* both; an empty half offers *Open a page here*, *Create new here*, *{page} on a phone* and *Work on {page} alone*; the edge between the halves is dragged (or moved with the keys) from a fifth to four fifths, kept per browser. *Desktop and phone* (the
editor's bar) draws the same page on both sides, the second at a phone's size: one editor drawing two canvases, a change on either on both, each canvas its own screen size, the settings editing the size of the canvas last pressed. What is shown is the address (`?with=` a document, `empty` or `mirror`; `?first=empty`; `?swap=1`; `DualLayout` in `src/lib/dual-page.ts`). `docs/dual-page.md` section 7.

Date: 2026-11-02

Asked for by the owner. *Why one editor for the same page twice:* two editors of one page would each hold their own copy, and the second save would undo the first; one editor drawing two canvases keeps one page, one undo and one save. *Why closing and swapping only rewrite the address:* the editors stay as they are, so nothing typed is lost; only opening a document needs the server. *Why a saved template is always the second half:* it has no editor address of its own, so it is drawn on the side
asked for with `?swap=1`. *Why the share is the first document's:* a swap keeps each page's width. *Why scrolling by share at two sizes:* the same part is in view on both, where pixels from the top would not be.
