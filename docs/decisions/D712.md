# D712: A/B guardrails as designed, and a sign-up goal for Kaizena Flow's store

**A/B guardrails as designed, and a sign-up goal for Kaizena Flow's store (D148 phase 19, `docs/ab-testing.md` "What phase 19 built"; 17 November 2026, `src/lib/experiment-guardrails.ts`, `guardrailCounts()` in `src/server/experiment-results.ts`, `recordSignup()` in `src/server/experiments.ts`).** Every A/B test is watched by four guardrails whatever its goal, each waiting for its minimum per version: **orders** (visitors who ordered, 1,000, `harmedVersion()` as since phase 1), **speed** (page
loads slower than 2.5 s to show their main content, 400; the speed beacon now says which versions the page showed and `recordSpeed()` keeps in `speed_samples.ab` only those the visitor's own A/B cookie confirms for a running test, never a visitor id; only while the store counts visits, else the guardrail says *Off*), **payments at checkout** (of the visitors who started checkout, those with a failed payment and no paid order, 200) and **refunds and returns** (of the buyers, those with a return
asked for or a refund made within 30 days of the order, 100). The first three stop a test on the strict interval (z 3.29) and, for speed and payments, a floor (five and two in a hundred more), and the hourly check keeps which in `experiments.stop_guardrail`; refunds and returns are said, never a stop, *still coming in* until 30 days after the stop.
The guardrail email (English and Norwegian), the test's page (the verdict's line, a warning for returns and *What keeps it safe* in the details), the AI manager's `explain_results`, the platform's flags and the platform assistant's advice name the guardrail. **A sign-up to Kaizena Flow** (`signup`) is a goal only Kaizena Flow's own store has (`HOUSE_GOALS`, refused elsewhere), first and chosen on its new-test form with clicks and forms; the sign-up form counts it once per exposed visitor in a
running sign-up test from the store's A/B cookie, only with statistics, never tying the request to the visitor. The AI manager's tools offer `TOOL_GOALS` (all but `signup`), so their schema and the eval recording are unchanged; the Admin API's and the `experiment.answered` webhook's `goal` can be `signup` (contract 1.37). Kaizena Flow's own store is the platform's side of both: its tests get the same guardrails, and the sign-up goal is the platform's alone.

Date: 2026-11-17

Asked for by the owner ("start phase 19. add Signing up as a goal on the plans page"). *Why the floors:* with thousands of loads a version a difference of one slow load in a hundred is beyond chance and still nothing a visitor feels; stopping on it would end good tests. *Why failed payments:* the design said "checkout errors"; the shop records failed payments and nothing else that goes wrong at checkout. *Why not stop on returns:* they arrive up to 30 days after an order, after most tests end.
*Why the sign-up is counted by the form and not from the ad conversions (D523), as `docs/ab-testing.md` phase 22 first said:* those need marketing consent and keep a request; a test needs statistics and keeps a version, so the count is the test's own. *Not done:* the plans-page test itself (the owner's), and a speed guardrail for a store that does not count visits.
