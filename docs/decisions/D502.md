# D502: Services bought from abroad, booked with reverse charge

**Services bought from abroad, booked with reverse charge (subscriptions step 1, 8 November 2026, `REVERSE_CHARGE` and `reverseChargeLines()` in `src/lib/purchases.ts`, *Bought abroad* in the VAT choice of *Add the receipt* and *Add the bill*).** The owner: most of Human Web's costs are licences and subscriptions (Claude, Vercel, Supabase and the like), to be budgeted, forecast and registered with their invoices.
Step 1 of four: such a service, bought from a seller abroad without Norwegian VAT, has its VAT reckoned by the buyer (*omvendt avgiftsplikt*, the VAT Act § 11-3).
The whole of what was paid is the cost, in kroner as the bank line shows it (the card's own rate, so no exchange rate is guessed), and 25 % of it is posted as VAT due on 2704 and the same deducted on 2714, both on code 86: it nets to nought, but stands on the VAT return (the base, the VAT due and the same as a deduction, as `vatReturnOf()` already reads codes 81 to 92) and in SAF-T (`lineVat()` reckons a reverse charge on its base).
Entertainment bought abroad owes the VAT and keeps it as a cost (code 87, no deduction). A bill from abroad is posted the same against 2400 and kept with `supplier_invoices.reverse_charge`; its amount is the kroner it costs, so a subscription paid by card is best added as the bank line's receipt. The model's reading preselects *Bought abroad* when a receipt or bill is in another currency than the books' and shows no VAT (`boughtAbroad()`); the owner can always change it.
A seller that charged Norwegian VAT through VOEC (because the business gave no organisation number) is not reverse charge, and that VAT is not deductible: the business gives the seller its number. Audited with `abroad`. Migration: `supplier_invoices.reverse_charge`. Next steps: (2) a register of subscriptions that matches the card charges each month, (3) invoices forwarded by email to the store's address, (4) budget against actual and a forecast on Books.
