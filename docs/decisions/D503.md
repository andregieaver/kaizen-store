# D503: Subscriptions

**Subscriptions: a register that knows their charges in the bank (subscriptions step 2, 8 November 2026, `commerce.recurring_costs`, `src/lib/recurring-costs.ts`, `src/server/recurring-costs.ts`, `RecurringCostsCard` on Settings → Books).** Step 2 of the owner's ask (D502): what Human Web pays again and again (Claude, Vercel, Supabase…) kept as a register on the Books screen.
Each subscription has a name, a word its charge carries in the bank's statement (ANTHROPIC, VERCEL), what it was (a purchase kind), its VAT or *Bought abroad* (D502), every month or every year, about what it costs in the books' currency, and since when. The card says what they cost a month and a year (a year's a twelfth), each one's last charge and the next, and one whose charge has not come a week after it was due (*No charge since …*).
A waiting bank line carrying a subscription's word (`chargeOf()`: whole words, on a day it ran, the nearest usual amount where two match) opens its receipt form with the subscription's name, kind and VAT filled: the charge is still posted only as a purchase with its receipt (D490), since the Bookkeeping Act wants the voucher.
Charges the statements show coming back each month (`likelySubscriptions()`: the same words without digits or the bank's own words in three months or more, at most about once a month, each within a quarter of the usual amount, the last within six weeks) are offered with *Add it*, as software at its usual VAT from the first charge, for the owner to change; a transfer made many times a month is never offered.
Owners add, change and end (audited `work.recurring_cost_added`, `_changed`, `_ended`); members who read settings see the card. Nothing in the register is posted, and it is never copied to another store. The VAT choice of receipts and bills now reads *Bought abroad* with a note under it (D502's longer words were cut off in the field). Kaizena Flow's own store has the same card (D466).
Next steps: (3) invoices forwarded by email to the store's address, (4) budget against actual and a forecast from the register.
