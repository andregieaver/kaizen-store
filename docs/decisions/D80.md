# D80: The site's header and footer are built in the page builder, from site components, for Kaizen and for each store; a header can lie over the top of pages that start with a background

**The site's header and footer are built in the page builder, from site components, for Kaizen and for each store; a header can lie over the top of pages that start with a background: every page, the front page, or pages in chosen page categories or tags.** The header's and footer's pieces are one block kind, `site` with a `part` (`SITE_PARTS`: logo, menu, phone menu button, search, account or sign in, wishlist, cart, countries, business or private switch, Start your store, business details,
cookies link), each with only its own settings (`SiteBlock`: the logo's height, which menu and whether its links go side by side, the countries as a list to open or as links, hidden on phones); `SitePartView` (`src/components/site-parts.tsx`) draws them with the store in its country, or with Kaizen's settings, from the same pieces as the standard header and footer. A store's parts (search, wishlist, cart, countries, buyer switch) are a store's; Start your store is Kaizen's.
Headers and footers are pages of type `header` and `footer` (no address, categories or tags; site components are refused anywhere else, and a footer must keep the business details and the cookies link), edited under Headers and Footers with the same editor; new ones start as the standard ones (`defaultHeader()`, `defaultFooter()`).
The site uses the one chosen on its list (`stores.header_id`/`footer_id`, `platform_settings.header_id`/`footer_id`, checked to be the owner's own of that type, let go when deleted), as last published; without one, the standard header and footer as before. For headers the builder gains three general options: row layouts whose side columns fit their content (`fit-sides`, `fit-middle`, `fit-end`), rows that keep their columns side by side on phones, and columns whose components sit side by side.
A header's `overlay` says where it lies over the page; a page the rule covers and whose first row has a background marks itself (`data-header-overlay`, `headerOverlays()`), and the stylesheet then takes the header out of the flow, see-through with its own text colour while the page is at its top, and with its background again once scrolled.
Pages visited before stay in the document, hidden, after client navigation, marks and all, so once scripts run the stylesheet follows `html[data-overlay-on]`, set by the shown page's `HeaderOverlayMark` and taken off when it is hidden; the page's own mark serves before (and without) scripts.

Date: 2026-09-27

Asked for by the owner. The same pattern as product layouts (D79): one block kind with parts, layouts as pages so drafts, publishing, translations, previews and copying to new stores come with them, and the choice as columns the database keeps to the owner's own. Keeping the standard header and footer until one is chosen changes nothing for sites that do not use it; the browser tests also pass with the standard layouts chosen.
The overlay is only over a first row with a background, since that is what it is for and it would otherwise cover the page's first words; pages mark themselves so the rule follows client navigation with no knowledge of addresses in the layout. A footer must keep the business details and cookies link, as the law and the cookie choices (D58) ask.
