# D116: Campaigns can be limited per customer and to countries, a "buy N pay for M" can stack, and the chat agent shows their offers

**Campaigns can be limited per customer and to countries, a "buy N pay for M" can stack, and the chat agent shows their offers.** *Per customer* (`campaigns.per_customer_limit`, 1 for once per customer): counted from the customer's own non-cancelled orders through `order_lines.campaign_parts`, only for a signed-in customer (`runningCampaigns()` gives none to someone who is not, since it cannot tell who has had it), locked in `placeOrder()` with the limited campaigns; the product pages still
announce it, with "Sign in to My account to get it" (`CampaignNotice.signIn`). *Countries* (`campaigns.markets`, market codes; none, or all of the store's, is every country): `runsIn()` in the engine's caller and in the notices; a free product still needs its amount for each country it runs in. *Stacking a "buy N pay for M"*: a stacking one works over the units still to be paid for after the campaign that won and the stackers before it, each unit free at most once (`freed` per line in
`applyCampaigns()`; a line's remaining value is spread evenly over the units left to pay for, and the cheapest of every full group of N are free), so two 3 for 2 on six items free three, not two; percentages and multi-buys stack in the order the campaigns were made, and a free product cannot stack (the database keeps `stacks` to percentages and multi-buys). *Chat* (`ChatProduct.offers`, `offersOn()` in `src/server/chat-agent.ts`): cards the agent draws carry the offer badges (two at most, never
a gift, as on the storefront's cards), and the `search_products` and `get_product` results give the model the offers as the site words them (`Name: 20 % rabatt`, a gift and its sign-in hint on a product's own page, and "taken off in the cart"), so it states no offer of its own; group campaigns are told to no one. Not yet: a limit per customer over a period, and a stacking free product.

Date: 2026-09-29

Asked for by the owner as the remaining follow-ups to D114 and D115. Counting a customer's orders from the same parts that count a campaign's uses, and locking the same rows, keeps one rule for both limits; announcing a per-customer campaign with a sign-in hint, rather than hiding it, tells shoppers what they must do to get what the page says.
