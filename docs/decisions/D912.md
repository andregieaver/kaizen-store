# D912: Plans: a product kind shown in a pricing table, never on a page of its own, that gives access in an app

Date: 2026-11-27

**A product can be a plan (`products.kind = 'plan'`): access to an app or a service, sold only by subscription, its variants its billing
periods (D911). A plan has no product page and no place in any list; a store's page shows it in the Plans component (D142), the pricing
table Kaizena Flow's own `/pricing` has, where *Choose plan* puts it in the cart.** Asked for by the owner while setting up the Kaizena Life
store: plans "shouldn't really have their own product page, rather than be plans shown in a pricing table", and "other stores may need the
same kind of plan setup, with the ability to approve access to a store owner's own app, third-party or otherwise."

*Why a kind, not a flag:* a plan differs from goods in what it is, not only where it shows: delivered as access (no shipping, file or
stock), never sold once, bought one at a time, and to be linked to the app it opens. A kind carries all of it, as a gift card's does (D300).

*Why the Plans component, not a new one:* it already draws monthly and yearly prices, the switch between them (D481) and a comparison
table. One component draws Kaizena Flow's plans on its pages and a store's plan products on the store's (the parity rule).

- **Step 1, built:** the kind and its database rules (`check_plan_product()`; a plan needs no picture), the editor's *A plan* (Monthly and
  Yearly made for the owner, *What's included* as the description, one thing per line), plans out of every list through `OFFERED`
  (`PLAN_OFFERED` for the pricing table alone), the pricing table on a store's page with *Choose plan* to the cart (one plan a cart, one of
  it), and a plan's old address leading to the pricing page. In full: `docs/plans.md`.
- **Step 2, designed, not built: access in an app.** Flow keeps each subscriber's access (who, which plan, until when) from the subscription;
  a plan names the app it opens, an app installed in the store (D441) through the owner's consent to a new scope `access:read`, a third
  party's or the owner's own (*Connect your own app* makes a private one); the app is sent signed `access.*` events and may read
  `/api/admin/v1/access` for its own plans only; a signed hand-off from the app carries its user's reference into the subscription, so it
  need not match by email. Kaizena Life becomes the first such app. In full: `docs/plans.md`.
- **The platform:** Kaizena Flow's own plans have had their pricing table since D142 and give access to Kaizena Flow itself, sold through
  Stripe Billing on the platform's account, not as products: nothing is missing there.

Idle cost: none per request or run. Two deferred constraint triggers (`products_plan`, `product_variants_plan`) read one product row per
write of a product or a variant; a page with the Plans component reads the store's plans once an hour per market (cached with the
catalogue); a missing product page asks once whether its handle is a plan's.
