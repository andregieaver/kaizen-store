# D43: A page is rows of columns of blocks, built by dragging

**A page is rows of columns of blocks, built by dragging.** The page editor has three columns: a left sidebar with four tabs (Components, Rows, and Sections and Layers, which are placeholders for later), the canvas, and the page's settings on the right (title and address first). Rows is a set of layouts: 1 to 6 equal columns, a left sidebar (1:2), a right sidebar (2:1) and both sidebars (1:2:1); dragged onto the canvas, a row lands before or after the row it is dropped on, or last.
Components holds Rich text, which is dragged into any column, before or after a block there. Rows and blocks already on the page are dragged by their handles (blocks into any column of any row), moved with Up and Down, and deleted (asking first when they hold text); a row's layout can be changed, and when it gets fewer columns the blocks of the columns that go move to its last column.
Without a mouse, pressing a row adds it at the end and pressing a component adds it to the column last worked in (or a new row), and every column has its own "+ Rich text". On the site each row's columns sit side by side from tablet width, sized by the layout, and stack on phones; empty rows and blocks are left out, and pages are now as wide as the header (1024 px).

Date: 2026-09-25

Asked for by the owner. Stored as `rows` in the page's JSON (`PageRow`, `PageColumn`); pages saved before rows (a list of blocks) read as one full-width row and are stored as rows at their next save, so no data was migrated. At most 50 rows and 100 blocks per page. Dragging uses dnd-kit (D42).
