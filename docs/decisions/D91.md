# D91: More page components, each added through one registry

**More page components, each added through one registry: separator lines, dual buttons, accordions, tabs, FAQs, videos, HTML, testimonials, social media buttons and icon lists, and carousels.** A newer kind of component is a block type (`PageBlock`, its schema, `newBlock()`, `blockHasContent()`, `blockText()`, `PageBlockView`) plus an entry in `BLOCK_EDITORS` (`src/components/admin/block-fields.tsx`: its title, General and Style fields); the builder's generic dialog adds the font, spacing, frame
and Advanced tab they share, and its texts are listed with labels in `mapBlockTexts()`, from which translating builds its fields. Components made of items (tabs, questions, testimonials) edit them with `ItemsEditor`; each item keeps an id, by which its texts' translations are kept. A separator line is an `hr` of its own style (solid, dashed, dotted, double), thickness, colour (the site's border colour unless chosen), width (10–100 % of its column) and place.
A dual button is two links side by side (`dualButton`), each with its own text, address, new tab, style and colours, sharing size, corners, weight, the space between and their place; one under another on phones if set, and each shown once it has text and an address (`buttonShows()`).
An accordion's sections (`PanelItem`: a title and rich text) are the browser's own `details`, so they open without script and the browser's find opens the one holding a word; the first can start open, one-at-a-time is a shared `name`, and it looks like lines between or boxes. Its words are the page's (`blockText()`: excerpt, llms.txt, the chat agent's knowledge).
Tabs share the accordion's items and follow the ARIA tabs pattern (`TabsView`: arrow keys, Home and End choose as they move focus); every panel is in the page, the others hidden, so search engines read them all; they look underlined, as pills or as boxes, placed at the start, centred or spread across.
FAQs (`faq`) are questions and answers drawn as the accordion is, a question shown once it has an answer (`faqShows()`), with schema.org's FAQPage (`faqJsonLd()`) beside them unless switched off, for search engines and AI assistants.
A video (`video`) is one uploaded to the site (`VideoUploadButton`, played by the browser with or without its controls; one that starts by itself starts muted, loops, and stays still for visitors who prefer less motion) or one on YouTube or Vimeo, given by its share address (`embedUrl()`, `src/lib/video-embed.ts`).
An outside video is a facade until pressed: its poster (a picture from the site, never fetched from YouTube or Vimeo) or a plain panel, a play button saying where it plays from, and only then the player, from youtube-nocookie.com or Vimeo with `dnt=1`, so nothing reaches either before the visitor chooses to play and no consent is needed. Its shape is a ratio (16:9 unless chosen) and its title names the player for screen readers and is translated.
HTML (`html`) is the owner's own code, styles and scripts (up to 50,000 characters), run in a sandboxed frame without `allow-same-origin` (`HtmlFrame`, `src/lib/html-frame.ts`): its own origin, so nothing in it reaches the site's or the admin's cookies, storage or session, which is why it may run on stores before they have their own hosts where the owner's page code (D61) may not.
A script in the frame reports its content's height, which the frame takes (up to 10,000 px) unless a height is set, and the page sends it its font and text colour. Content from another service can wait for the visitor to press Show, so nothing loads before they ask. Its code is the same in every language; only its title, naming the frame, is translated, and none of its words are the page's. On the builder's canvas frames take no pointer, so the block is chosen and dragged as any other.
Testimonials (`testimonials`) are what customers said, written in by the owner (words, name, title or place, 1–5 stars, a picture), drawn by `TestimonialCards` as cards, plain or large quotes in one to four columns (one on phones): each a `figure` whose `blockquote` is what was said and whose caption is who said it, the stars read out as words (`StarRating`). What was said and who they are are translated, a name never; the words are the page's.
Set to Google (`source: "google"`), they are the business's rating and reviews on Google (`GoogleReviewsSection`, `src/server/google-reviews.ts`): the owner's Google Maps Platform key, encrypted like payment secrets, and their business, found and chosen by name, are kept per owner in `commerce.google_places` (a store's under Integrations, owners only; Kaizen's under Platform), and the reviews are asked of the Places API (New) as the page is shown, in its language, inside `<Suspense>` and never
kept, as Google's terms allow no caching; each page view showing them is one request on the owner's Google account. Only the business's id and the language are sent. Google is named as their source with a link to the business on Google Maps, each reviewer's name links to their profile there, and reviewers' pictures are left out, as loading them from Google would tell it about the visitor.
A component can show only reviews with enough stars, and fewer than Google's five; Google's words are not the page's.
A content grid or testimonials can show as a carousel (`display: "carousel"`, `Carousel`): the same list in one row that scrolls sideways with the browser's own scrolling, snapping to a tile (`[data-carousel-track]` in globals.css), as many tiles to a screen as the columns (a grid can show a quarter of the next, `peek`), with previous and next arrows moving a screenful and off at either end.
Nothing moves by itself, touch and trackpads scroll it, and tabbing to a tile's link brings it into view, so no tile is out of reach without the arrows.
Social media buttons (`socialLinks`, `SocialLinksView`, `src/lib/social-links.ts`) link to the business's profiles on thirty networks, plus email, phone and website: each its logo (Simple Icons, CC0, in `BrandIcon`, LinkedIn's drawn in since Simple Icons dropped it), named for screen readers or in words, opening in a new tab with `rel="me"`; as icons, filled or outlined circles, rounded or square, in each network's colour (black marks take the text's, so they show on dark themes; filled ones get
black or white by `textOn()`), the theme's accent or one chosen. What the owner types becomes the address (`socialHref()`: https added, an email address as `mailto:`, a number as `tel:`), and only such addresses are kept.
An icon list (`iconList`, `IconListView`) is lines of words, each after one of 66 icons (`ICONS` in `src/lib/icons.ts`, drawn by `ListIcon` from Lucide, ISC, imported by name so only those ship), one under another or side by side, in the theme's accent or a colour chosen, a line a link if given an address; the icons are hidden from screen readers, as the words say what each line means, and the words are the page's and translated line by line.

Asked for by the owner, with tabs, accordions, dual buttons, HTML, FAQs, testimonials, video and a carousel. One registry keeps a component's editor in one place instead of eight across a 4,700-line builder, and translation from the texts' own list means no component can be forgotten there.
