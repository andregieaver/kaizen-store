# D658: The paperwork for an AI that acts on its own

**The paperwork for an AI that acts on its own (`docs/100-day-challenge.md` F7; 16 November 2026, `docs/ai-act.md`, `docs/dpia-ai.md`, `src/lib/legal-starters.ts`, `src/lib/platform-legal/`).** The AI Act classification rested the autopilot's minimal risk partly on "changes that matter wait for a person's yes", and neither it nor the DPIA said that in challenge mode the autopilot runs a store on its own, nor that a store's customer service may be answered by an AI before a person reads it.
`docs/ai-act.md` now says what the autopilot does alone in challenge mode (prices above the margin floor, campaigns, broadcasts and answers within budgets, a signed-in customer's own address change, stock within a budget, every change watched and undone after a clear fall) and why the class does not move: none of it is a decision about a person in Annex III's sense, and money back to customers, escalations and what is never automatic still go to a person.
`docs/dpia-ai.md` 4.1 and 4.5 say the same for people's data.
Shoppers are told: while a store's desk works AI first (`chatWidgetFor()`: an AI is set up and the mode is `ai_first`), its privacy starter gains `privacy.automated.answers`, hand-written in nb, sv, da and en: a message in the chat or by email may be answered by an AI on its own, about the reader's own order when it can tell it is theirs, a request they confirm (a new delivery address before anything is sent) carried out, its answers labelled, refunds and exceptions decided by a person, and a
person on request. Kaizena Flow's own desk works AI first, so its privacy notice says the same in section 10 (en and nb), beside "the AI features suggest; a person decides", which still holds for every decision with legal or similar effect. The lawyer's confirmation of both documents is still to come.
