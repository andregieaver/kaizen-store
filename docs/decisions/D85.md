# D85: Menus are the owner's own named lists of links, edited on a Menus page modelled on WordPress's menu editor and shown wherever they are chosen; links can sit under links

**Menus are the owner's own named lists of links, edited on a Menus page modelled on WordPress's menu editor and shown wherever they are chosen; links can sit under links.** A store and Kaizen each have any number of menus (`commerce.menus`, unique names per owner, at most 100 links, three levels).
The editor adds links from the site's pages, products, categories, tags, articles and blog categories (ticked in a list, searchable), the site's own links (front page, all products, blog, account, cart; Kaizen's sign-up and sign-in) or a custom address with its text; the links are ordered by dragging, dragged right to sit under the link above, or moved with Up one, Down one, Under …, Out from under … and To the top; each opens to change its text per language, open in a new tab, or be removed
(the links under it move up into its place). A link keeps its item's depth under the ones before it, so a menu is a flat list in order (`MenuEntry.depth`) made a tree where it is drawn (`menuTree()`); a link to something not published or gone is left out, the links under it taking its place.
The editor knows nothing of places (WordPress's Menu settings are left out): the standard header (and the phone's slide-out menu) and footer show the menus chosen under Header and footer (`header_menu_id`, `footer_menu_id`, which let go when the menu is deleted), and a Menu component in any page, header or footer shows the one it names, side by side (links under a link in a list opening below it on hover or keyboard focus, no script) or one under another.
The editor lists where each menu is used. Each site's header and footer menus became menus of their own (Main menu, Footer menu), and headers' and footers' menu parts became Menu components showing them; new stores get copies of the template's menus, used where the template's were.

Date: 2026-09-28

Asked for by the owner, with WordPress's editor as the model. Menus as data of their own, chosen where they are shown, let one menu serve the header, a footer and a sidebar alike, and let pages have menus too; a flat list with depths keeps the stored form, the editor's moves and the checks simple, and a tree is only needed to draw it. Lists that open on hover and focus need no script, so prerendered pages keep working without it.
