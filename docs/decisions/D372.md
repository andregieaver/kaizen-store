# D372: Partial fulfilment with a carrier, and a second parcel of the same order booked straight after the first

**Partial fulfilment with a carrier, and a second parcel of the same order booked straight after the first (wave 10 unit I, `docs/wave-10-must-haves.md` 4); built.** The refusal "This order was just booked with Bring" was Kaizena Flow's own: `bringBook()`, `helthjemBook()` and `porterbuddyBook()` each refused any booking of an order that had a parcel of that carrier from the last two minutes, a double-click guard (D134) that also refused the second parcel of a partly sent order.
Bring never refused one: its Booking request (`bookingBody()`) carries nothing of the order (no reference, no correlation id), so there was nothing of Bring's to make unique.
The window is gone, replaced by what every carrier booking now shares (`src/server/carrier-booking.ts`): one booking of an order at a time, under a transaction-scoped advisory lock taken without waiting (`oneBookingAtATime()`, `carrier-book:{order}`; a second booking while the first is with the carrier is told *Another booking of this order is being made right now*, `BOOKING_BUSY`), the parcel checked under the lock before the carrier is asked or paid (`bookingPrecheck()`: `parcelPrecheck()`,
and with `seen`, the order's fulfilment as the page showed it, `OrderFulfilment.basis`, a press made before the page showed the last parcel is refused as `changed`), then `markSent()` with the parcel's lines as before. The order page's three booking forms send `seen` (`BringBooking`, `ChosenDeliveryBooking`, `basis`).
Reading the carriers' requests found the real cause of the same failure elsewhere: Porterbuddy's booking sent the order's number as its `Idempotency-Key`, so Porterbuddy would have answered a second parcel of the order with the first parcel's booking.
A booking now carries `BookingRequest.parcelReference` (`parcelReference()`: the order's number for its first parcel, `{number}-{n}` for the n-th, undone parcels counted, read under the lock so a retry of a failed booking keeps its key), which Porterbuddy's adapter sends as the key; Helthjem's `reference` stays the order's number (it is a reference, not a key).
Integration tests book part of an order with each carrier (a parcel naming only its lines and units, the order `paid` and *Partly sent* after it, the rest booked straight after as its own parcel and the order sent, a stale `seen` refused before the carrier is asked, nothing left to send refused before the carrier is asked; Bring also two bookings at once, the second told to wait; Porterbuddy each parcel's own key against a fake that answers a repeated key as Porterbuddy does):
`bring-shipping.int.test.ts`, `helthjem-shipping.int.test.ts`, `porterbuddy-shipping.int.test.ts`. **Objectives (REL-09, 30 October 2026):** each part has a service level objective over the last 30 days, worked out from the same checks (`SLO_TARGETS`, `sloOf()`): 99.9 % for stores and websites, sign-in and payments, 99.5 % for email and background work (which retry by themselves); the page shows the share measured, met or missed, and the minutes of failed checks still allowed (a check every five
minutes, `CHECK_MINUTES`).

Date: 2026-10-28

The parity row's gap named two things: no carrier booking of part of an order was tested, and two Bring parcels of one order could not be booked back to back. The spec said a second booking "waits and retries"; there was nothing at Bring to wait for, so the guard that caused it was replaced instead, keeping what it was for (no parcel paid for twice by a double press or a second tab). No migration.
