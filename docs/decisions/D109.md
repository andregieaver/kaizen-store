# D109: Language and currency are chosen apart

**Language and currency are chosen apart: a store offers any languages and any currencies, whichever countries it sells to, and a shopper picks both.** A *market* stays a country (shipping, VAT, prices), but the address now says how it is shown: `/s/{store}/no` is Norway in its own language and currency, `no-en` English, `no-eur` euro, `no-en-eur` both (`src/lib/market-slug.ts`: two letters are a language, three a currency, a country's own left out, so every old address still works and each view
is one cacheable, indexable address).
A store's languages are `stores.locales` (the main one first, one variant per language; never fewer than each country's own default language, which the owner sets per country in `markets.default_locale`), its currencies `commerce.store_currencies` (a rate in units per 1 EUR, as the ECB publishes them, a rounding step and an order); until an owner chooses, both are what its countries have, so nothing changed for existing stores (`localizationOf()` in `src/lib/localization.ts`).
Prices, shipping, free-shipping limits, discount amounts, fees and every other stored amount stay in each country's own currency, and are converted when read (`Market.conversion`, `shown()` in `src/lib/markets.ts`; `convertedSql()` in `src/server/product-conditions.ts` for filters, sorting and search, which use the same factor and step as the code): `Math.round(amount × rate(to) / rate(from) × 10^(digits(to) − digits(from)) / step) × step`, in minor units, the euro being the pivot.
A currency without a rate cannot be converted into or out of, and is not offered; converting to a country's own currency needs no rate.
`Market` carries what is shown (`currency`, `locale`) and what is kept (`nativeCurrency`, `ownLocale`), so the ~300 places that read `market.currency` and `market.locale` show the shopper's choice without change, and code that writes an amount down (a subscription, a weekly delivery) reads `nativeCurrency`; subscriptions and weekly deliveries are only sold in a country's own currency (shown nowhere else, `needsNativeCurrency`, and the deliveries page redirects to the native view).
The cart, checkout and order all record the currency shown, `cartSummary()` and `placeOrder()` convert the same way and Stripe is sent that currency (`checkout-kinds.int.test.ts` takes goods, a stay, an appointment, a rental, a code and a company's discount through checkout in euro and holds cart, order and Stripe together); a code's fixed amount and a shipping rate are converted, and a booking's price is worked out in the country's own currency first and then converted once.
The header (and the phone's menu and the footer) offers a language and a currency choice (`ViewMenu`, `src/components/view-menu.tsx`, links to the same page in the other address), the canonical address is always the country's own view, and every language of every country is a `hreflang` alternate (sitemap, page head, Open Graph `alternateLocale`).
Owners set it at `/admin/{store}/settings/localization` (owners change it, staff see it): languages with the main one and each country's default, currencies with rate and rounding, and "Keep the rates up to date" (`rates_auto`), which the daily cron (`refreshAutoRates()`, from `src/lib/ecb.ts`'s parser of the ECB's daily XML) applies to stores that ask, or "Fetch the ECB's rates now".
Anything that was `store.markets.map(m => m.locale)` (translations, menus, product texts, alt texts, chat knowledge, SEO texts) now reads `store.localization.locales`, so a store can write and translate in English without selling to an English-speaking country.
In the page builder's right sidebar *Translate with AI* (`TranslateWithAi` in `page-editor.tsx`, `src/lib/page-translate-ai.ts`, `src/server/page-translate.ts`, `translateStorePageAction`, feature `page_translation` in the usage reports) translates the page as it stands in the editor, saved or not, into one other language or all of them: only the texts not translated yet, or everything again over the translations there are.
Rich text is sent as its runs of formatting and put back into the same document, so bold and links stay where they were.
The model gets the page's words (with what each is, and its longest length) and no prices or stock; each answer is checked for its shape, its length and the claims filter (`findClaims()`: a translation with more claims, prices or urgency than the source is left out, and the text stays in the main language, which the panel says), and the result is only put into the page as a draft translation for staff to read, change and save.
Emails about an order link back to the language and currency it was placed in while the store still offers them (`context()` in `src/server/shopper-emails.ts`), else to the country's own address. No place falls back to a currency of its own: a store's main currency is its own country's (`mainCurrency()`), Kaizen's plan currencies list its own country's first, and a store with no country yet gets euro or a clear message.
Not yet: a rate per market's own currency entered by hand for a single product, and other decimal separators than the language's own in the admin's amount fields. *Since D178 step 4 (`docs/store-features.md` 4d):* other languages and currencies than a country's own are offered only while Several languages and Several currencies are on (`Localization.languageChoice`/`currencyChoice`; with Several currencies off a country is not shown in another country's own currency either); an address asking
for one not offered moves (308) to the same page in the country's own address, and what was bought keeps opening as it was (`resolveAfterSaleShop()`); the rates stay for history.

Date: 2026-09-29

Asked for by the owner: language and currency must be separate choices, with a store owner offering various currencies whatever the language and various languages whatever the currencies, plus a *Translate with AI* in the page builder.
Keeping every price in the country's own currency and converting when reading, at rates the owner controls (or the ECB's), keeps VAT, shipping and price history exact and gives a shopper in Norway euro without a second price list to maintain; the rounding step keeps converted prices looking like prices. The choice lives in the address so each view is cacheable and indexable, needs no cookie (`KNOWN_COOKIES` is unchanged), and the old addresses stay as they were.
Translations are drafts because a model may be wrong and a store answers for what it publishes.
