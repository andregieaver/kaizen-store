# D89: The media library's pictures have alt texts in each of the site's languages, written by staff or by the site's AI looking at the picture, and the site shows them wherever a picture has none of its own

**The media library's pictures have alt texts in each of the site's languages, written by staff or by the site's AI looking at the picture, and the site shows them wherever a picture has none of its own.** `media.alt` is the owner's main language's (a store's first market's, English for Kaizen), `alt_translations` the others by locale, `alt_source` who wrote them (`ai` or `staff`; the AI never writes over staff's unless someone asks on that picture).
The AI (`writeAltText()`, `src/server/alt-texts.ts`) is the site's text model (D73) sent the picture's small copy as bytes (`imagePart()`), the site's name, its languages and where the picture is used, and asked for JSON; each text is cleaned and passes the claims filter with colour words allowed (`parseAltTexts()`, `src/lib/alt-text.ts`), a language with a claim left out.
It runs from the library (one picture, or every missing one in batches with progress), and for new pictures in the five-minute cron (a few per site; a picture it could not describe waits a day).
The site uses them wherever a picture has no alt text of its own: products, their variants and the cart through `commerce.media_alt(url, locale)` (by either address, the market's language else the main one), pages, articles, headers, footers and product layouts through `withLibraryAlts()` in their loaders.
Each use in the library also shows its address on the site (the store's own domain from its settings, P8, and its main market): the product, page or article, or the front page for a header, footer, logo or sharing picture. Image sitemaps list every picture on a page, not only its own picture.

Date: 2026-09-28

Asked for by the owner: alt texts matter to people who cannot see the pictures and to search engines and AI assistants. Kept on the library item rather than copied into each page and product, so one text serves every place a picture is used and a change reaches them all; a page or product that describes a picture itself still wins.
Saved without review because the owner asked for it and the texts are short, grounded in the picture and filtered for claims; they are marked as the AI's and can be changed at any time.
