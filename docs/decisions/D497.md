# D497: SAF-T Financial from our own books

**SAF-T Financial from our own books (B5 step 2, 8 November 2026, `src/lib/saft.ts`, `src/server/saft.ts`, Settings → Books → *SAF-T file*).** A year of the store's open book as Skatteetaten's SAF-T Financial file, the one it asks for in an audit and the one an accountant moves books between systems with: *Download 2026* for each year the book holds (only its days: a book opened on 1 November gives November and December, and says so).
The file is schema v1.30 (mandatory for periods from 2025), v1.40 named for periods from 2027; it uses only what both have, and the validated sample (`src/data/saft/saft-sample.xml`, xmllint against both schemas, github.com/Skatteetaten/saf-t) is held to the writer by a unit test, and a file written from the integration test's rows validated too.
In it: the company (the books' organisation number, else the store's, as the VAT return; without one no file is offered; the store's postal address in its parts where it reads `Storgata 1, 0150 Oslo`), who to ask (the member who made it, the store's contact email), the selection by months; every account of the chart with its code of Skatteetaten's business statement (*næringsspesifikasjon* 2025–2026, its own list kept as `src/data/ledger/no-grouping.json`, the bank, insurance, IFRS and
petroleum codes left out) and its balances at the start and end, a balance sheet account carrying all before it and a profit and loss account only its year's, an earlier year's result no entry carried to equity put on other equity (2050) as the opening balance from another system does, so either end sums to nought; the customers and suppliers the lines name with what each owed at the start and end (a Work invoice's, credit note's or payment's client; the shop's buyers, paid at checkout, as one
customer, *Nettbutikkens kunder*, as a cash sale is kept; a bill's supplier, and its payment's; a reversal's by the entry it reverses; a line by hand or the opening balance names none); the VAT codes used (`TaxTable`, our codes being the standard codes); and the entries of the days, a journal for each series, each line debit or credit, a line with a VAT code carrying its VAT (`TaxInformation`: the entry's VAT for the code shared by the bases' lines, the rest on the last; a reverse charge's
reckoned on its base, since its due and deducted halves net to nought), the VAT account's own line none, as Skatteetaten's example has it. **No ready mapping from the standard accounts to the business statement's codes exists**: ours (`NO_GROUPING_RANGES`) was made account by account from the names of both lists, the statement's own number where an account has it and means the same (1500, 1920, 2400, 3000), else the code that says what the account holds (2700 to 2769 VAT owed, 2740, as
Skatteetaten's example; 1579 other receivables, 1570; 6800 and 6900 office costs, 6995; 7770 bank and card fees, other costs, 7700; 4300 goods bought, 4005); a unit test holds it to cover 1000 to 8999 without a gap and give every one of the 745 standard accounts a code that exists, and the Books screen shows each account's code (*How each account is reported*) for the owner's accountant to check. A download is a route, not a page, so the AI manager's prompt is unchanged.
Readers: anyone who sees the Books (Read-only is *look at everything*), each file audited (`work.ledger_saft_downloaded`, ids and counts). The platform: Kaizena Flow keeps no books of its own in the admin; the business behind it keeps them as a store (Human Web, the pilot; `docs/platform-parity.md`, the accounting row), and has the file there.
