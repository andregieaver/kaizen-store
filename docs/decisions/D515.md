# D515: The platform's events to its own endpoints, and *Add to Slack*

**The platform's events to its own endpoints, and *Add to Slack* (C4 of `docs/platform-parity-plan.md`, steps 1 and 2, 9 November 2026, `commerce.platform_webhook_endpoints`, `src/server/platform-endpoints.ts`, Platform → Settings → Integrations → *Your own endpoints*).** The owner: *start with 1 and 2* (the threshold watch, then C4). **Own endpoints:** a store's own endpoints (D354) for Kaizena Flow's events (D465: a store requested, made or opened, a plan started, changed, cancelled or
not paid, a notice of illegal content). An endpoint is a name, an HTTPS address that passes the address guard when saved and at each send, the events it gets and a signing secret of 32 random bytes shown once, address and secret kept encrypted; at most ten. Separate `platform_*` rows on D465's model, not the stores' tables widened: those hold `store_id` not null and every reader filters by it.
`commerce.queue_platform_event()` now queues one delivery (`platform_integration_deliveries`, `provider = 'webhook'` with its `endpoint_id`) for each endpoint that is on and asks for the event; `deliverPlatformDue()` signs and sends it with the stores' `postSigned()` (the same four headers and HMAC), the body as Zapier and Make get it.
A test, a delivery sent again with its id kept, a new secret, switched off by a platform owner or after three days of failures with none arriving (logged, said on the page), removed with its log. **Add to Slack:** Kaizena Flow's own Slack app (D101) for the platform's channel: the connect cookie carries `PLATFORM_SLACK` in place of a store's slug, the callback checks it is the platform owner who started it and keeps the channel's webhook as a pasted one is (`connectPlatformSlack()`).
Both are the settings area's (D510): a platform owner adds and changes, every role reads. **Next (C4 steps 3 and 4):** workflows on the platform's events, and an Admin API scope for its own tools (stores, plans, requests).
