# D108: Customer groups with a fixed discount, and company accounts whose employees are invited by email

**Customer groups with a fixed discount, and company accounts whose employees are invited by email.** A store makes *customer groups* (`commerce.customer_tiers`: a name and a whole percentage, 1–100; `/admin/{store}/customer-groups`), such as Wholesale at 10 %, and puts customers in one (`customers.tier_id`, from the group's page by email or from the customer's own page; someone with no account yet gets one made ahead of them, its email not proven, so they prove it when they sign in).
A *company* (`commerce.customer_companies`; `/admin/{store}/companies`) has a group, a share of the group's discount its employees get (`employee_share_percent`, 100 is all of it, 50 half; the main account always gets all of it), a limit on accounts (`max_members`) and an optional organisation number; a customer belongs to at most one company (`customers.company_id`, `company_role` `owner` for the main account or `employee`).
The discount is read from the membership on every purchase (`memberDiscountFor()` in `src/server/customer-tiers.ts`, the pure rule `memberDiscount()` in `src/lib/customer-tiers.ts`): the better of the customer's own group and their company's, none for a group or company switched off, so taking someone out of a company stops it at once.
It comes off everything bought once (goods, downloads, appointments, stays, rentals), not subscriptions, sign-up fees or shipping, as the lowered unit price a subscription's discount is (`planPrice()`), before any discount code, which then counts the lowered prices; free shipping still counts the basket at list price.
`cartSummary()` and `placeOrder()` work it out the same way (`checkout-kinds.int.test.ts` holds them together with a group's member and a company's employee); the order keeps its part of the discount (`orders.member_discount_minor`, `member_label`, `member_percent`, and `order_lines.member_discount_minor`), and Stripe takes it in the same one-time coupon as a code (named after the company or group, and the code when both).
A company's main account invites employees from My account, My company (`/s/{store}/{market}/account/company`: several addresses at once, each an email with a link; up to 20 at a time, 50 a day, the company's limit counting invitations waiting; sending to an address with an invitation open sends it again with a new link, and the old one stops working), sees its accounts and invitations, withdraws an invitation (`revokeInvite()`) or removes an employee (`removeMember()`) at any time; an employee
can leave. The link opens `/s/{store}/{market}/account/company/invite/{token}`, a page with a button: nothing changes until it is pressed, so an email scanner accepts nothing.
Accepting (`acceptInvite()`, one transaction under a lock) opens the account if there is none, or joins the existing one (an account whose email nobody had proven loses a password chosen before, as it may not have been theirs; a proven account keeps its password), proves the email (the link is what proves it), claims orders placed with it, and emails a confirmation with a link that signs in once (`customer_sign_in_links`, a hash kept, valid a week; its page `/account/sign-in/{token}` also uses a
button). An account already in another company here cannot accept until it has left that one. A company or main account emails the person when they are removed. Invitations are kept a year after they end, links a month after they expire (daily cron). Tokens are only ever kept as SHA-256 hashes.

Date: 2026-09-29

Asked for by the owner: a B2B store giving a group of customers a fixed 10 % discount by tier, and companies whose main account invites employees, who then get the same discount or a share of it, also if they already have an account, with the invitation revocable at any time as employees leave. Reading the discount from the membership on every purchase, rather than copying it onto accounts, is what makes revoking immediate.
Taking it off before codes and only from things bought once keeps subscriptions' own pricing and the sign-up fees as they are and lets the cart, checkout and Stripe agree to the øre. Buttons rather than links that act keep mail scanners in companies from spending invitations and sign-in links.
Not yet: prices shown per customer on product pages (the page says the discount is taken off in the cart), a separate discount per product or category, invitations restricted to the company's email domain, and the AI manager's tools for groups and companies.
