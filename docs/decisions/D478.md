# D478: Our own ledger, in shadow

**Our own ledger, in shadow (B2 of `docs/own-books-and-sales.md`), built in steps.** A double-entry journal kept by Kaizena Flow, run beside Tripletex for the pilot until three months agree to the øre on every account it posts to. **Step 1, the journal, its chart and its rules (built).** A book (`commerce.ledger_books`) is one legal entity's: its name and organisation number when it opened, its country (the pack: chart and VAT codes) and currency, its first day, a last day once closed, and the
day it is locked through; one open book a store, so a change of the business behind the store (D473) closes one and opens the next and two entities' books never mix (Human Web AS's open on 1 January 2027 from the ENK's handover).
Its chart (`ledger_accounts`) holds numbers that report as Skatteetaten's standard accounts (SAF-T's `StandardAccountID`, NS 4102's four digits, or a sub-account of one such as `19201` under 1920), each with its kind from its class and a default VAT code from the standard codes; both lists are Skatteetaten's published SAF-T files, read on 6 November 2026 and kept as they are (`src/data/ledger/no-saft.json`; low-rate output VAT is 2703, not 2702).
A new Norwegian book starts with 38 accounts: all Kaizena Flow posts to and what a small business uses most.
Entries (`ledger_entries`, `ledger_lines`) are written only by `commerce.ledger_post()`, which under the book's row lock refuses an entry that does not balance, has fewer than two lines or a zero, or a line on an account not active in its own book, or is dated outside the book's days or on or before its lock; numbers it in its series (`S` sales documents, `B` bank and payouts, `M` by hand, `IB` opening balances) and year without gaps; and posts what it was posted from once (the same document
again returns its entry, unless that was reversed). Nothing posted is changed or deleted: `commerce.ledger_reverse()` puts a mistake right with the signs turned, in the entry's series, on its own day while that is open, once, and never a reversal. A book keeps its store, country, currency and first day, closes once after its last entry and is never deleted; an account with postings keeps its kind.
`entryOfDoc()` posts a Kaizena Flow document (`BookingDoc`, the shape Tripletex and Fiken book from): the total on the receivable, each VAT bucket's net on its income account with its standard code (3, 31, 33, 5, 6, 52 for services abroad [U: the accountant's choice]) and each rate's VAT on 2700, 2701 or 2703; a credit note the signs turned; another currency than the book's waits (a rate is step 2's).
Where the store books in Tripletex, its own accounts are used (`postingOfTripletex()`), so the comparison is account by account. `ledgerTrialBalance()` reads each account's balance before a period, its debits and credits, and after, and the result. The ledger's audit entries are `work.ledger_*` (the owner's own bookkeeping, D122), ids only.
Tests: `src/lib/ledger.test.ts`, `src/db/ledger.test.ts` (every rule against Postgres), `src/server/ledger.int.test.ts`. **Step 2a, documents posted as they are issued (built).** The dispatcher's `ledger` job (`runLedgerPosting()`, 40 seconds) posts, for each store's books open or closed in the last 60 days, the documents issued in their days that have no entry: the same set Tripletex books (Work's sent invoices, not imported ones, and credit notes; the shop's invoices and credit notes), read by
the one loader both syncs use (`loadDocument()`), the oldest first. One with an entry, even one a person reversed, is done. One that cannot be posted as it is waits with why (`commerce.ledger_waiting`: another currency, a VAT bucket with no account, totals that do not add up, a locked day) and is tried again a day after it was last tried; the books' screen will list them. Before posting, a standard account the store's posting needs and its chart lacks is added.
Tests: `src/server/ledger-posting.int.test.ts` (a Work invoice posted once, one in euro waiting and tried again a day later, a reversed one left be, a shop order's invoice by the job). **Step 3, the screen (built).** Settings → Books (`/settings/books`, in the Advanced sidebar under Store; words a server-only area, `books`): before the books open, the owner chooses the day they start (by default the day Tripletex started booking, else the first of this month) and *Open the books*, which posts
the documents of their days at once; then the legal entity and its number, the day the books start and the day they are locked through, what waits and why, the trial balance of any days (this year by default) with each account's balance before, its debits and credits and its balance after, the totals and the period's result, and *Lock* through a day (the owner's; moving it back is audited). Members who may read settings see it.
In the admin's map, which the AI manager reads, it was first offered where the owner keeps Work (`needs: "work"`), so the manager's fixed drawing and its recorded eval stayed as they were; on 7 November 2026 it is offered to every store (a shop's invoices are posted too), with the reports and payments in its description, the manager's prompt drawn anew and the eval recorded again on Platform → AI.
E2e: `e2e/signed-in/books.spec.ts`. **Step 2b, a document in another currency (built).** It is posted in the book's currency at the rate it was issued at (`fx_rate`, as the document froze it), each line converted and rounded and the VAT lines made to add up to the VAT the document reported in the book's currency (`vat_home_minor`, the rounding on the largest), the receivable their sum; a shop's document only where its VAT's currency is the book's.
A document without a rate still waits. **Step 2c, first part: payments for Work invoices (built, 7 November 2026).** Each row of `work_invoice_payments` received in the book's days is posted once in the bank series (`B`) on its day by `entryOfPayment()`: the money where its method puts it (`PAYMENT_ACCOUNTS`: bank and other 1920, cash 1900, card and Stripe 1579 with the payment provider until its payout reaches the bank, a prepayment 2900) against the receivable the invoice was posted to; money
paid back the same with the signs turned. In another currency the receivable is cleared at the invoice's own rate and the money is worth the ECB's rate of its day (`rateOnDay()`, the last published on or before it), the difference an exchange gain on 8060 or a loss on 8160.
A reversal (`reverses`) turns the payment's own entry line by line (`entryOfPaymentReversal()`), so a mistake is taken back to the øre, and waits (`original`) until that entry is posted; a payment of an imported invoice is posted too, its receivable being in the opening balance. Tripletex's sync books no payments, so nothing is compared.
Tests: `ledger.test.ts` (accounts, signs, gain, loss, reversal), `ledger-posting.int.test.ts` (a payment, its reversal, a card payment, one in euro). **Step 2c, second part: the shop's money and Stripe's payouts (built, 7 November 2026).** A shop's captured payment (not a test one) moves its order's receivable to where the money is (`SHOP_PAYMENT_ACCOUNTS`: Stripe's to 1579 until its payout, one taken by hand to the till 1900 or the bank 1920), on the day staff said it came, else the day its
order's invoice was issued, else the day it was made (`SHOP_PAYMENT_DAY`); a refund that moves money (`money`, and `release` of a card amount never captured) turns that back on its day, one to store credit owes it to the customer (2900), and one that moves no money (`transfer` to an exchange order, `void` of an unpaid order) posts nothing, its credit note doing the work.
A paid payout (`entryOfPayout()`) is what reached the bank (1920) and what Stripe kept (7770, each line's fee and a fee of its own as a line) against the gross out of the provider's balance (1579), on its arrival day; one whose lines are not read yet waits (`lines`), a manual one is posted at its amount. So a charge, its refund and the payout that settles them leave 1579 at nothing.
Only in the book's currency for now (another waits, `currency`); a dispute's money stays on 1579 until disputes are posted.
Tests: `ledger.test.ts`, `ledger-posting.int.test.ts` (an order's invoice and card payment by the job, its refund, its credit note and the payout, 1579 back to nothing). **Step 2c, third part: gift cards and store credit spent (built, 7 November 2026).** Where a shop payment's money is, or a refund's goes, is one function for every way of paying (`shopMoneyAccount()`): Stripe's on the provider's balance (1579), one taken by hand in the till or the bank, a gift card's off what cards owe
(`GIFT_CARD_ACCOUNT`, 2900, where the invoice's `voucher` line put the card sold), store credit's the same (it is a card, D364), and a refund to store credit owed on 2900; an exchange's payment moves no money and is never due; Mollie, a payment at a venue and one on terms wait (`provider`, said on Settings → Books) instead of being posted to the bank as before, where a card's payment would have gone too (none was in production).
So a refund to store credit and the credit spent on a later order leave on 2900 exactly what the card still holds (`ledger-posting.int.test.ts`).
A card given by staff (source `staff`) is not posted as it is given yet, so its spending takes 2900 below what was sold until that step. **Step 2c, fourth part: shop money in another currency (built, 7 November 2026).** A shop payment or refund in another currency than the book's clears the receivable at its document's own rate (the order's invoice, a refund's credit note, both carrying the rate into the book's currency for VAT, D157) and puts the money where it is at what it was worth in the
book's currency (`shopMoneyRates()`): a Stripe payment at what Stripe settled it for, its payout's line, so it waits (`lines`) until that payout is read; one taken by hand at the ECB's rate of its day; the difference an exchange gain (8060) or loss (8160), `PaymentRates.moneyMinor` in `entryOfPayment()`. A gift card or store credit in another currency still waits (`currency`): what cards owe stays at the rate they were sold at.
Tested on a euro order paid by card whose payout settled 25.00 kroner above the invoice's rate: the receivable and the provider's balance both end at nothing. **Step 2c, fifth part: disputes (built, 7 November 2026).** A dispute's money is posted as the paid payout's line shows it (`entryOfDisputeLine()`, source `dispute`, on the line's day): taken back from the provider's balance (a negative line), the order's receivable open again, as the customer has not paid after all; given back when the
dispute is won (a positive line), the receivable cleared again. The dispute's fee is the payout's, with its other fees (7770). So a disputed charge, its dispute and the payout leave the provider's balance at nothing; a dispute lost leaves the receivable open, for the owner to write off as a loss with the accountant (not yet a line in *Needs you*). Only in the book's currency for now.
Step left of 2c: cards given by staff. **Step 2d: the opening balance read from Tripletex (built, 7 November 2026).** On Settings → Books, while the books have none and Tripletex is connected, the owner presses *Bring in the opening balance*: Tripletex's balance sheet (`GET /balanceSheet`, `readTripletexBalances()` in `src/server/ledger-opening.ts`) is read for the day before the books open, and `openingBalanceOf()` makes the entry: each balance sheet account (classes 1 and 2) at what it held,
and when the books open during a year each profit and loss account at what it moved since 1 January, so the year's result goes on; on 1 January the balance as that day began. Tripletex's own trial balance must balance to the øre, else nothing is posted and the owner is told by how much. A result of earlier years never carried to equity there is what is left, and goes on 2050 as a line of its own, said to the owner with the advice to check it with the accountant.
An account of Tripletex's own that the chart lacks is added with its name, reporting as the standard account nearest below it (`nearestStandard()`: 1921 as 1920). Posted once in the opening balance's series on the day the books open (source `opening_balance`), audited (`work.ledger_opening_posted`), first in the journal; reversed there, it can be read again.
Tests: `ledger.test.ts`, `ledger-opening.int.test.ts` (1 July with the year's sales and costs and a second bank account; posted once; refused when an øre off; read again after a reversal; 1 January with last year's uncarried result on 2050), with the Tripletex stand-in's balance sheet.
Fiken's opening balance is left for a store that keeps its books there. **Step 3b, first part: the reports (built, 7 November 2026).** Settings → Books shows, for the same days as the trial balance, the profit and loss (`profitAndLoss()`: NS 4102's classes as `RESULT_GROUPS`, 30–35 sales to 83–84 tax, income and costs both positive, the operating result, net financial items, the result before tax and for the period, which is the trial balance's), the balance sheet on the last day
(`balanceSheet()`: fixed and current assets, equity, long- and short-term debt, and the result of every day so far not yet carried to equity as a line of its own, so the two sides agree; a mismatch says so) and VAT by code (`vatByCode()`, `ledgerVatByCode()`: each SAF-T standard code's base from its income and cost lines and its VAT from the 27xx lines, sales and output VAT positive, with the VAT to pay), with the two-month VAT term the last day is in (`vatTermOf()`) one press away.
Tests: `ledger.test.ts` (groups, results, a balanced sheet, VAT by code, terms), `ledger-posting.int.test.ts` (VAT by code of two invoices, one in euro), `e2e/signed-in/books.spec.ts`. **Step 3b, second part: entries by hand and the journal (built, 7 November 2026).** The owner posts an entry by hand on Settings → Books (one step away, behind *Post an entry*): its kind (an entry by hand, series `M`, or the opening balance, `IB`; never the series documents are posted in), its day, what it is,
and up to eight rows of an account from the chart, a debit or a credit and a VAT code where it has one (`handEntryOf()`: an empty row left out, a row with both or neither said, an amount read as typed in the book's currency with no floating point, then the entry's own rules, balanced to the øre), posted by `ledger_post` as every entry is and kept in the audit log (`work.ledger_entry_posted`).
The journal lists the latest 30 entries, newest first, each with its lines, what it was posted from (or *By hand*), and what reversed it or what it reverses; the owner reverses one from there (`reverseLedgerEntry()`, once, on its own day while that is open, else today).
Tests: `ledger.test.ts` (rows read, problems by row, series), `ledger-posting.int.test.ts` (an unbalanced entry refused, an opening balance posted, in the journal, reversed once), `e2e/signed-in/books.spec.ts` (posted after a refusal, found in the journal, reversed).
Step 3b is done. **Step 4, compared with Tripletex (built).** Each document posted here that the store's Tripletex sync booked there (its row `synced`, with the voucher's id) is read back as the sync reads it (`GET /ledger/voucher/{id}`, its postings' account numbers and amounts) and compared (`compareWithTheirs()`): every account but the VAT group by its amount without VAT, and the VAT as one total (here the VAT accounts' lines, there what Tripletex worked out of each gross amount, which holds
whether or not it lists its VAT postings apart). The result is kept (`commerce.ledger_comparisons`: `agrees`, `differs` with each account's two amounts, or `missing` when the voucher is gone); one that does not agree is compared again a day later. The dispatcher's `ledger` job compares after it posts, and Settings → Books says how many agree, differ or are gone, and what differs, by the øre. Reads only from Tripletex.
Tests: `src/lib/ledger.test.ts`, `src/server/ledger-compare.int.test.ts` (a document that agrees, one whose voucher was changed in Tripletex by an øre, one whose voucher was deleted).
The pilot's exit is three months in which every document agrees. *Needs your attention* (the owner's list on the home, `attentionFor()`, `StoreFigures.books`) says how many documents differ from Tripletex while their entry stands (one reversed since is not counted) and how many have waited more than a week to be posted (a payment waiting for its payout is posted within days), both one press from the books (7 November 2026). Kaizena Flow's own books are its own store's (D467).
