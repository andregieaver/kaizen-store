# D384: Moving in from another shop

**Moving in from another shop: WooCommerce's product file, redirects from old addresses, order history (wave 11 unit E, `docs/wave-11-core-and-psi.md` 2.E): built.** WooCommerce's own product export (its CSV import schema at github.com/woocommerce/woocommerce/wiki/Product-CSV-Import-Schema, read 29 October 2026) is read by `readWooCommerce()` (`src/lib/product-csv-woocommerce.ts`, told from Shopify's by its columns, `isWooCommerce()`) into the product import's neutral rows, so it is checked,
planned and saved as Shopify's is (D165: dry run, the VAT question, `safeFetch()` pictures, `saveProduct()`, never a compare-at price): a variable product is one product with its variations (found by `id:` or SKU in any order), its options their attributes; the price is the sale price while its dates hold, else the regular price; categories keep their `>` levels; Published 1 is active, anything else a draft; grouped and external products and a variation without its parent are skipped and said;
every column not read is listed with why.
A product the import makes leaves a manual redirect from its old shop's address (`oldProductAddress()`: Shopify's `/products/{handle}`, WooCommerce's `/product/{slug}`) to its new one, through `addRedirects()` (origin `import`, an address already redirected skipped) in the name of whoever started the import, read again (`passMembership()`, `website:write`, else a finding says why none was made); *Make redirects from the old addresses* is on by default (`ImportOptions.redirects`).
Shopify's order export is the D165 job kind `order_history_import` (`src/lib/order-history.ts` reads it, `readShopifyOrders()`: an order over its rows, the amounts in its currency, paid, fulfilled or cancelled, the addresses; payment, notes, phone, tags and risk never read; what cannot be read named by its rows, nothing guessed; at most 20,000 orders of 200 lines; `src/server/order-history-import.ts` on the customer import's pipeline and views, Orders → Export → *Import order history*,
`/admin/{store}/orders/history-import`).
Each order is written only by `insertHistoryOrder()` in `src/server/order-insert.ts` as a copied order (D129): `copied_from` a key of the store and the order's name, number `C-{name}` outside the series, the store's own country, the file's total with the subtotal worked out so it adds up, lines at their prices with no variant and no VAT of their own, a `copied` event; so nothing pays, refunds, sends, emails, invoices or counts it (the triggers and readers that pass copied orders by), and the
database keeps it unchanged.
The owner's alone, as the order file is (D165); the log carries counts only. **Course changes.** (1) An order is tied to a customer by the order's email matching one the store already has, not by the customer import's own rows: the import keeps no link from a row to a customer, and the email is the only key both files share; nobody is made or subscribed, and the page says to import customers first. (2) WooCommerce's file has no address column, so a product's handle is its name as WordPress makes
a slug of it (`wooSlug()`), the address its shop had unless an owner changed the slug by hand (then the redirect points from an address nobody used, harmless). (3) A sale price's dates are judged on today's UTC date. (4) An order number already taken in the store (a store copy's `C-…`) counts as imported and is left.
