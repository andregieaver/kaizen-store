# D911: A purchase option can be for one variant, so monthly and yearly are variants with their own prices

Date: 2026-11-27

**A purchase option (D25, `selling_plans`) can be for one of its product's variants (`selling_plans.variant_id`, null for every variant).
Monthly and yearly are then two variants with their own prices, each with its own option, and a shopper can never mix them.** Asked for
by the owner while setting up the Kaizena Life store: "rather than adding monthly and yearly as separate products, shouldn't these be
added as subscriptions with monthly and yearly as options?" Until now an option's price could only be the product's price less a whole
percent: one product at 199 kr a month and 1 990 kr a year meant "1 990 kr, monthly at 90 % off", advertised as a discount it is not; and
variants could not help, because every option applied to every variant (the yearly variant could be bought every month).

*Why variants, not a price on the option:* a price kept on the option would be a second way to set a price, beside the one rule that prices
change only through `commerce.set_price` (VAT-inclusive per market, the 30-day prior price, price lists, campaigns, currencies). A variant
already has all of that. So the option keeps its discount, trial, sign-up fee and commitment, and only learns which variant it is for.

- **The database:** `selling_plans.variant_id` (`selling_plans_variant_fk` to the store's variant, `selling_plans_variant_idx`), always
  one of its own product's variants (`selling_plans_variant_of_product()`). Products go with their variants and options together. Both
  store copies keep each option on the copy of its variant: `clone_store()` and `duplicate_store()` are wrapped (the originals are now
  `clone_store_rows()` and `duplicate_store_rows()`, never called directly), each copied row named `clone_id(new store, old id)` as before.
  Migrations `20261127114122_amused_virginia_dare`, `20261127114220_purchase_option_variant_rules`; `src/db/purchase-option-variant.test.ts`.
- **The editor:** with more than one variant, each option has *For: every variant / a variant* (by SKU, as a download file's variant is,
  and following a SKU that changes). *Add a purchase option* on a product whose variants are billing periods takes the next variant without
  an option, at the rhythm its name says (`rhythmIn()`: Monthly, Årlig, Jährlich…) and its own price. The checks: an option names a variant
  the product has on; two options renew alike only for different variants; a product sold only by subscription has an option for every
  variant on. An option is never widened to every variant when its variant is gone.
- **Shoppers:** the product page offers the options the chosen variant can be bought with (`plansFor()`; `PurchaseOptions` now inside
  `VariantChoice`), choosing the first for a product sold only by subscription; an option whose variant is off is not offered. The price
  beside the title is the lowest a subscriber can pay (`lowestPlanPrice()`); in lists, options each for one variant count at their smallest
  discount, so no list shows a price below what can be paid. Adding to the cart, the cart, checkout (`plan_ok`) and the variants a running
  subscription can switch to all refuse an option with a variant it is not for.
- **Kaizena Life (another product):** its store sells Plus as one product with the variants Monthly (`life-plus-monthly`) and Yearly
  (`life-plus-yearly`), and Running Analyzer likewise; Life's plans read the SKU, so its four `plan_prices.flow_sku` stay as set.
- **The platform:** Kaizena Flow's own plans already price each interval on its own (`plans`, `plan_prices`, D18), so nothing is missing
  there (the parity rule).
- **Not done:** switching a running subscription from monthly to yearly (a change of variant and of option at once, with Stripe's
  proration) stays a cancel and a new purchase.
