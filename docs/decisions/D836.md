# D836: Each person changes their own name and email: the name on Your account, the email after a link to the new address, the old one told

**A person changes their own name and email, and a platform admin can correct a customer's (20 November 2026).** Until now both were set at sign-up or invitation and never again. Your account opens with *Name and email*, each with *Change*: the name is a field (trimmed, 1 to 120 characters, `cleanName()`), `accounts.name`, read at once by the Team page, the change log and the platform's customers.
The email's *Change* opens `/admin/account/email` (one line: a link goes to the new address), with the waiting change, *Send the link again* and *Cancel the change* there. Asking needs the second step where the account has one and a sign-in in the last ten minutes (`freshness()`), as deleting an account does; no password, as many accounts have none. The address must be free (said without saying whose).
The change waits in `commerce.account_email_changes` (migration `20261120004843_account_email_changes.sql`, RLS on): the address and a SHA-256 of 32 random bytes, one per account, 24 hours. The link goes to the new address through `sendEmail()`, its token never in the email log (`secrets`), `log()` or the change log; the old address is told, the new one half hidden. Five links an hour per account (`email_change` budget, failing closed).
The link's page (`/admin/account/email/confirm`, outside the gate) changes nothing by being opened and signs nobody in; its button runs `confirmEmailChange()`: one transaction locks the row and the account, checks the link, moves `accounts.email` and Kaizena Flow's own customer of the person while it still matches, marks the link used, and last calls `adminAuth().admin.updateUserById(id, { email, email_confirm: true })`.
Supabase refusing rolls it all back and the person is told; an account with no Auth user yet moves its row alone. Then the old address is told, and the person's apps and AI assistants ask again (`endAccountGrants(…, "email")`). Platform parity: Platform → Customers corrects a name (stores area) and, for an owner after a fresh sign-in, sends a link (never sets the address), both in the platform's change log.
A store owner does not rename a member: the account is theirs across every store. Not changed: a theme's author name, a domain's registrant, the plans' Stripe account (keyed by the store's account). Follow-up: the account's own Stripe customer for domains (`accounts.stripe_customer_*`) keeps the old address until Stripe is told. The safeguards in full are `docs/security.md` 2.4.
Files: `src/lib/email-change.ts`, `src/server/email-change.ts`, Your account, `account/email/page.tsx`, `src/app/admin/account/email/confirm/page.tsx`, the platform's customer page; words in en and nb. Tests: `email-change.test.ts`, `email-change.int.test.ts` (16), `commerce.test.ts` (D836), `e2e/signed-in/account-details.spec.ts`.
Idle cost: none per request (Your account reads one indexed row); per scheduled run, one delete in the daily job; one table, empty unless someone is changing their email.
