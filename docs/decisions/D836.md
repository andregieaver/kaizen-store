# D836: Each person changes their own name and email: the name on Your account, the email after a link to the new address, the old one told

**A person changes their own name and email, and a platform admin can correct a customer's (20 November 2026).** Until now a name and an email were set at sign-up or invitation and never again. Your account opens with *Name and email*: the name and the email, each with *Change*. The name opens a field (trimmed, spaces in a row and unprinted characters taken out, 1 to 120 characters,
`cleanName()`) saved with `ActionForm`; it is `accounts.name`, so the Team page, the change log and the platform's customers read it at once. The email's *Change* opens `/admin/account/email`, whose one line says a link goes to the new address and the email changes when it is opened; the waiting change (sent when, works until, sent by an admin), *Send the link again* and *Cancel the change* are there, one step away.
Asking needs a strong, fresh session (`freshness()`): the second step passed where the account has one (or where that could not be read), and a sign-in in the last ten minutes for everyone, as closing a store or deleting an account asks; *Sign in again* comes back to the page. No password is asked, as many accounts sign in by link and have none. The new address must be an address,
not the account's, and no account's at all, said without saying whose (`account.email.problems.taken`). The change waits in `commerce.account_email_changes` (migration `20261120004843_account_email_changes.sql`, one file, RLS on by `.enableRLS()`): the address, a SHA-256 of the link's 32 random bytes, who asked, created, expires (24 hours) and used; one per account, a new request replaces it and its old link stops working.
The link goes to the new address through the platform's own mailer (`sendEmail()`, kind `security.email_change_confirm`, words in the person's admin language), its token kept out of the email log (`secrets`, so a platform reader of Platform → Emails cannot use it), out of `log()` (ids only) and out of the change log; the old address is told a change was asked for, the new one half hidden (`maskEmail()`, `security.email_change_requested`).
Five links an hour per account and 300 for the site (`budgetRoom(null, "email_change", accountId)`, failing closed), counted on the person whoever asks. The link opens `/admin/account/email/confirm`, outside the gate, `noindex` and `no-referrer`: opening it changes nothing (a mail scanner confirms nothing), and nobody needs to be or is signed in by it; its button calls `confirmEmailChange()`.
One transaction locks the pending row and the account, checks the link (known, unused, unexpired, the account active, the address still free), moves `accounts.email`, marks the link used, moves the person's customer of Kaizena Flow's own store while it still matches the account (its tags and notes with it, `updateCustomerIn()`, in a savepoint, so what staff wrote stays), and last sets Supabase Auth's email with
`adminAuth().admin.updateUserById(id, { email, email_confirm: true })`; Supabase refusing (or no secret key) rolls everything back and the page says nothing was changed, an `email_exists` reads as taken, a commit that fails after Supabase took the address puts Supabase back. Then the old address is told it was changed and what to do if it was not them (`security.email_change_changed`), and every app and AI assistant the person connected
asks again (`endAccountGrants(…, "email")`, as a new password does). A session is tied by `auth_user_id`, so it stays signed in. An account with no Supabase Auth user yet (invited, never signed in) moves its row alone and links by the new address at its first sign-in. Kaizen Life: a link is kept by account id and stays; signing in with Kaizen Life finds the Auth user through its identity, and `linkAccount()` matches the new address.
The platform (parity): Platform → Customers → a person has *Name and email*: the name corrected by the stores area (support and owners, `platformAction({ area: "stores" })`); a link to a new address sent, and a waiting one cancelled, only by an owner of the platform after their own sign-in in the last ten minutes, never for themselves and never setting the address; the person's current address is told that an admin asked.
Every step writes the change log for the platform and each store the person works in (`auditAccount()`, now with `changes`): `account.name_changed`, `account.name_corrected`, `account.email_change_requested`, `account.email_change_sent`, `account.email_change_cancelled`, `account.email_changed`, the field as `{ changed: true }`, never a name or an address as a value (an admin's entry names its person, as a two-step reset does).
A store owner does not correct a member's name: the account is the person's across every store they work in, so one store's owner renaming it would rename them for the others. Not changed, on purpose: a theme's author name (the author's own choice for the listing), a domain's registrant (the registrar's contact, changed at the registrar), the store's Stripe account billed for plans (keyed by the store's connected account, not a person's address).
Follow-up: the account's own Stripe customer for domains bought without a store (`accounts.stripe_customer_*`, made with the address and name by `accountCustomer()`) keeps the old address until Stripe is told; emails sent to an old address stay in the email log until their 12 months are up, as account erasure blanks only the current address's. Used and expired changes go a day later (`pruneEmailChanges()`, daily), and with the account.
