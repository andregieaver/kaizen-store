# D888: Back in stock stays a notice and Workflows stay staff automations; flows carry their triggers (email plan M2.2)

Date: 2026-11-24

**What M2.2 leaves out, and why (24 November 2026, email plan M2.2).** The plan asked for every automation to become a recipe on the flows
engine and for Workflows to share its editor. Most of it is done: welcome, after a purchase, win-back, cross-sell, restock and browse start
as flows from a goal (D767 slices 3 and 4). Two parts stay as they are.

- **Back in stock stays a notice, as cart reminders do (D33).** A shopper asks to hear once when a sold-out variant returns
  (`stock_requests`, D298); most who ask are not on the marketing list, and the notice is something they asked for, so the contact policy
  always lets it go (D764). Flows reach only people on the list and are marketing, held by the contact policy and the sending guard, so as
  a flow it would stop reaching the people who asked.
- **Workflows stay staff automations.** A workflow acts for the store's staff (email the owners, post in Slack, call an endpoint, tag an
  order) on any event of the log or a schedule (D356); a flow sends marketing emails to people on the list. Their triggers are now the
  flows' too: the events a flow may start on (M2.3), a segment entered (D887) and a schedule. One editor for both would mix staff actions
  into a marketing story, so each keeps its own page; a flow's canvas is the flows' editor.

Idle cost: none; nothing is added.
