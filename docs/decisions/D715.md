# D715: A/B tests, phase 20

**A/B tests, phase 20: going on to a page, forms elsewhere, new or returning visitors, preview links to share (D148, `docs/ab-testing.md` "What phase 20 built"; 17 November 2026, `src/components/ab/ab-page-goal.tsx`, `src/lib/ab-preview.ts`, `src/lib/ab-preview-sign.ts`, `src/server/ab-preview.ts`, `/api/ab/preview`, `ExperimentShare`).** The goal **going on to a page** (`page`): the share of a test's visitors who then open a chosen page of the store (published pages, the front page, the All
products page; never the page under test or a working page), counted once per visitor and only after they saw the version, by a marker drawn on the pages a running test names (`pageGoalTests()`, cached with the tests) through `/api/ab/event`. The **form goal** may name a form in the header, the footer or on another published page (`siteForms()`); a click goal stays on the page. **New or returning visitors** are chosen under *Customise* on the new-test form and on a draft.
A **preview link** per version of a running test (*Show it to someone*, one press, copied): a token signed with a key made from the server's secret for this use alone, good 30 days, kept a day in `kaizen_ab_preview` after the test's id in plain (`{test}:{token}`, so the browser's check decodes nothing) (necessary, listed); the proxy serves that version for that test with `noindex` and no caching; while a browser holds a good preview of a store the server reads no assignment for it, so nothing is
recorded; once the test stops the link lands on the store and keeps nothing; made with `marketing:write`, audited. The AI manager's tools keep `TOOL_GOALS` (no `page`), so their schema and the eval recording are unchanged; the Admin API's and the `experiment.answered` webhook's `goal` can be `page` (contract 1.39). Kaizena Flow's own store has all of it, the page goal behind *More goals* beside its sign-ups.

Date: 2026-11-17

Asked for by the owner ("start phase 20"). *Why counted only after the exposure:* "went on to" means after seeing the version; a page opened first says nothing about it. *Why a form elsewhere counts by its id wherever it is sent:* a footer's sign-up is on every page, and the visitor who saw the version may send it from any of them. *Why a previewing browser records nothing for the whole store:* one rule at the one place every recorder reads the visitor (`readAssignments()`), rather than a check
in each; a preview lasts a day. *Why returning means the browser already held the store's A/B cookie:* it is what the engine knows without keeping anything else about a visitor.
