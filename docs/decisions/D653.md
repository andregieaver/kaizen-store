# D653: Flows of several emails, cross-sell and restock

**Flows of several emails, cross-sell and restock (`docs/100-day-challenge.md` D4 step 2; 16 November 2026, `src/server/automations.ts`, `src/lib/automations.ts`, migration `20261116013445_cold_lorna_dane`).** The three lifecycle automations sent one email each and could not show a reader anything about their own order. Two kinds join them, both a set number of days after delivery: *Goes well with* (`cross_sell`, 1 to 90 days, 14 to start) and *Time to restock* (`replenish`, 7 to 365, 30).
Their products are chosen per reader by code, never by a model (the AI grounding rule): `picksFor()` takes, for cross-sell, what other paid orders of the last year had with the order's products, most often first, leaving out any product the address has bought; for restock, the order's own products that one address bought in two paid orders in the last two years (`rebought`), and an order without such a product starts no restock email at all.
Both keep to products on sale and in stock now (`OFFERED`, `inStockNow()`), at most three (`PICKS_SHOWN`), placed into the owner's words before the first button by `withPicks()` and drawn per market by `EmailFacts` like any product block; an email with no products is claimed `suppressed` and never goes.
Every lifecycle kind may also have up to two follow-ups (`commerce.automation_steps`, positions 2 and 3, 1 to 60 days after the email before), sent to the same person for the same moment only while they are subscribed and have placed no paid order since the flow's first email; the send's key is the first one's with `#{step}` and `automation_sends.step` says which, so each goes once.
Follow-ups are edited one step away from the automation (Lisa's depth; Homer's view is unchanged): a Follow-ups card on its page, each on its own page with the same editor, test and delete (the third moves up when the second goes). The starter words are hand-written in four languages and never name a product.
Browse abandonment, the third flow the plan named, is not built: it needs a record of which products a known person looked at, and the store keeps none (visits are an anonymous daily hash, D152), so it waits for its own privacy decision. Store-only: Kaizena Flow's own list gets no lifecycle automations (D298 kept them to stores; the platform sells plans, so cross-sell and restock have nothing to show).
The AI manager's tools came in the next push, kept apart so the migration did not wait on a new eval recording: `write_email_automation` names the new kinds and takes a `step` (2 or 3 writes a follow-up by the same writer, told it is a later email to those who have not ordered since; a missing one only as the next, after the first email was saved), and the cart reminders' steps: `cart_reminder_stats` now lists them too, `write_cart_reminder` (the manager's own words in the main language refused
for claims or the brand's avoided words, a discount code only one the store has, other languages keeping theirs) and `switch_email_automation` with `cart_reminders`, every write behind the `send` gate and in the autonomy area `marketing`.
Only one tool is new: a turn sends at most 128 tools (D333), and the marketing and website areas with the core tools must fit in one, so the cart reminders joined the automation tools rather than having their own, and removing a follow-up stays on the owner's screen. Migration version `20261116013445_cold_lorna_dane` went first, with no tool change, applied by CI with the deploy of `bcea116` (16 November 2026); advisors after: INFO only.
