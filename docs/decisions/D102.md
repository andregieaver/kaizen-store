# D102: Subscription boxes

**Subscription boxes (first built as weekly deliveries): signed-in shoppers keep a standing list, which becomes each delivery's order at the cutoff and is charged to their saved card when the delivery is sent.** A module stores switch on under Features (`deliveries` in `stores.modules`). The store sets delivery days (`delivery_schedules`): one weekday in one market, and a cutoff 1–7 days before at a time in the store's time zone.
A shopper starts from Weekly delivery in My account (`/s/{store}/{market}/deliveries`): they choose a day, give the address and tick that their card is charged for each delivery when it is on its way, for what it holds at that day's prices, until they end it; Stripe Checkout in setup mode on the store's connected account then saves the card (a Stripe customer there, never `payment_method_types`), and the list (`standing_orders`, one open per customer) is on, confirmed by email.
Products go on the list from their pages (a second button beside Add to cart: the store's own goods to ship, bought once) and are changed on the list page (quantities 1–99, at most 100 kinds).
At each cutoff, the five-minute job turns every list agreed to before it into that delivery's order (`standing_deliveries`, once per list and day): its lines capped to the stock there is, placed through `placeOrder()` from a cart of its own, so prices, VAT, shipping and stock locks are the checkout's; its stock is held until the day after the delivery, and the shopper is emailed what it holds, what it costs and what was left out.
A day skipped, a paused list or an empty one is recorded without an order. A change made after a cutoff first makes that delivery (as the list stood) and then goes into the next, so a delivery being packed never changes. The order waits for payment until staff mark it sent: Mark sent then charges its total to the saved card off session, with Kaizen's fee, and only a successful charge marks it paid and sent.
A refused card (or a bank asking the shopper to confirm) leaves the order waiting and emails the shopper a link to pay by Stripe Checkout, whose card then becomes the list's; an open link is closed before staff try the card again, so nothing is paid twice. Shoppers skip coming days, pause, resume or end the list (ending lets go of the card; a delivery already being packed is still sent and charged), and staff cancel a delivery not yet sent without a charge.
Deleting the customer account deletes its lists and cards; the orders stay.

Asked for by the owner for a grocery box. Stripe Billing needs a fixed amount per period, so a list that changes every week is charged by Kaizen with Stripe's saved-card payments (as the no-show fee, D66), which also lets the charge be what was packed. Charging when it is sent follows the grocery practice of paying for what arrives. Prices are the day of the cutoff's, and the shopper is told the amount at the cutoff, before anything is charged.
Shoppers and staff read it as a subscription box (Abonnementsboks, Prenumerationsbox), a name that fits any goods, not only groceries; the code and tables keep the delivery words.
