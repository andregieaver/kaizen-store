# D79: Product layouts

**Product layouts: a store lays out its product pages in the page builder, with product components for the product's parts, and chooses where each layout is used: for single products, for product categories and tags, or as the store's standard.** The product page's parts are one block kind, `product` with a `part` (`PRODUCT_PARTS`: back link, pictures, title with the wishlist heart, price, business-only notice, host, buy, description, right of withdrawal, product safety, and related products,
new), each with only its own settings (`ProductBlock`) and defaults that draw the page as before; `ProductPartView` (`src/components/product-parts.tsx`) draws them with the product, the buy part adapting to its kind (goods with stock and subscriptions, appointments' times, stays' and rentals' dates), and a part with nothing for the product leaves no space.
Layouts are pages of type `product_layout` (a store's only, no address, categories or tags; product components are refused anywhere else), edited under Product layouts with the same editor, whose palette then offers the product's parts as sketches on the canvas; a layout's preview shows it with any of the store's products.
`productLayoutFor()` picks the product's own layout, else its nearest category's (a category's counts for those below it), else a tag's, else the store's standard (`stores`, `terms` and `products.product_layout_id`, checked to be the store's own layouts, let go when one is deleted), and only published ones; without one, the built-in `DEFAULT_PRODUCT_LAYOUT`, the page as it was. The page's structured data is drawn once, outside the layout.

Date: 2026-09-27

Asked for by the owner. One block kind with parts keeps the page builder's many per-kind lists to one entry; the parts are the page's existing pieces moved, so the standard layout is the page shoppers had (the browser tests pass unchanged). Layouts are pages so drafts, publishing, translations, saved parts and copying to new stores come with them; assignments are columns rather than a table so the database keeps them to the store's own layouts and lets go on delete, as with front pages (D54).
`clone_store()` copies layouts as it copies published pages, and their uses. A category's layout counting for subcategories follows how category listings include them (D50).
