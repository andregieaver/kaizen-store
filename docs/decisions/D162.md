# D162: GDPR export, erasure and a retention schedule

**GDPR export, erasure and a retention schedule (wave 1, unit 1g).** A register (`PERSONAL_DATA`, `src/lib/personal-data.ts`) names every table that can hold a person's data, how it links to them, what the export says and what erasure does; three detectors (by column name including `*_name`, by foreign key to a shopper anchor, by free-text or payload column) fail `src/db/privacy.test.ts` for any table not in it, so a later wave cannot add personal data unnoticed.
`exportCustomerData()` gives one JSON file (profile, addresses, orders with lines, payments, refunds with their reasons, shipments, invoices and credit notes, returns and withdrawals, subscriptions and standing lists, wishlists, bonus, referrals, consents held under the person, emails, carts, custom fields) to staff (`customers:write`, a POST) and to the shopper after a fresh sign-in (10 minutes, five downloads an hour); the file is never emailed and never holds another store's or another
person's data. A shopper's export and erasure act only on the account's own proven email, never on an address typed on an order.
`eraseSubject()` closes the account and removes or restricts every register entry: orders under the bookkeeping duty are restricted (`orders.restricted_at`, cut loose from the person, unused, never emailed, relinked or copied) and anonymised by `commerce.anonymise_order()` through the daily retention run after the seller's country's period (NO 5, SE 7, DK 5, DE 8, other 10 years from the end of the calendar year of the sale), unpaid and copied orders are cancelled and anonymised at once after
the Stripe session is closed, subscriptions are cancelled, email bodies are blanked (rows kept for their keys), the email opt-out is kept; nothing deletes an order (D141) and invoices change only through `anonymise_expired_documents()` (D159).
`commerce.retention_rules` holds every period with source, basis, date and a reviewed flag (written only by `set_retention_rule()`, five-year floor for bookkeeping); `runRetention()` runs daily from the subscription-reminders cron in application code with a clock argument and ends the gaps found in this read (customer codes and sessions, email bodies, carts, quotes, webhook payloads, orders).
`commerce.privacy_requests` carries the one-month clock of GDPR Art. 12(3), extension to three months and a reasoned refusal. Every export and erasure is audit-logged with counts and ids only. All consumer wording is `src/lib/privacy-text.ts`: hand-written nb, sv, da, en, needing legal review. Retention periods are unverified by an accountant.
