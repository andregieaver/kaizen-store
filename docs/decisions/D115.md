# D115: Campaigns are announced on product pages and cards without slowing them, can be limited to customer groups and to a number of orders, can stack, and the AI manager runs them

**Campaigns are announced on product pages and cards without slowing them, can be limited to customer groups and to a number of orders, can stack, and the AI manager runs them.** *Notices* (`src/lib/campaign-notices.ts`, `src/server/campaign-notices.ts`): `campaignNotices(storeId, market)` is a `'use cache'` read tagged `campaigns:{storeId}` (`campaignsTag`), so a product page or card gets its campaigns like it gets the product: from the cache, no read per request, in the prerendered shell (a
new `campaigns` product part, in the default layout after the price and for owners' own layouts to add; a badge of the offer on the price, two at most, on product cards, in `ProductGrid` and content grids; a free product is told on the product's page only).
The cache lasts to the next start or end of a campaign, at least a minute and at most an hour, with the expiry kept past five minutes (a shorter one would make the notice a dynamic hole, out of the prerendered page), and a minute while a campaign has a limit; the admin's save, delete and switch refresh the tag (`updateTag` in the actions, `ctx.invalidate` in the AI tools).
`CampaignEnds` (a client component on `useSyncExternalStore`) hides an announcement the moment its campaign ends, so the cached page never promises what the cart no longer gives. Only what is the same for everyone is announced: never a campaign for chosen customer groups, and a limited one only while it has orders left.
Measured locally on the production build (curl, 40 requests each, median): product page, All products, front page and category page answer in ~4 ms to first byte and ~21–31 ms in all, the same with three campaigns running as without any; the first visit to a page not prerendered (which reads the notices once) took 100–240 ms. *Limits*: `campaigns.usage_limit` (orders in all: an order counts once however many lines got it, pending payment counted and a cancelled checkout giving its use back,
as a code's; `placeOrder()` locks the store's limited campaigns while it counts, so two checkouts cannot take the last use), `tier_ids` (only customers in those customer groups, their company's group included, D108: `customerTierIds()`; needs a signed-in customer), and `stacks` (a percentage that, instead of competing, comes off what the campaign that won left, one after another in the order they were made, `applyCampaigns()`; only for percentages).
Each order line keeps every campaign that gave on it (`order_lines.campaign_parts`, `[{ id, name, minor }]`, the first being `campaign_id`), which the list's orders and given figures read. *The AI manager*: `list_campaigns`, and `create_campaign` and `set_campaign_active` gated as `public` (kept for the owner's yes, with an `approvalSummary()`); a campaign is made from words (products by title or handle, categories, tags and customer groups by name, days as 2026-10-01, a free product by SKU, its
amount in the main country's currency), and served to Kaizen Life's MCP with the other owner tools; the "run a sale" playbook names them. Not yet: a limit per customer, campaigns for a country only, and stacking a 3 for 2 with a second 3 for 2.

Date: 2026-09-29

Asked for by the owner: the notice on product pages and cards, keeping pages under half a second to load; tools for the AI manager; limits to customer groups and a number of uses; and stacking.
Reading campaigns through the catalogue's own cache, with a lifetime that follows the campaigns' dates and the page ending an announcement itself, is what keeps the shell prerendered and the time to first byte where it was; leaving group campaigns out of the notices keeps a cached page from saying what only some can have.
