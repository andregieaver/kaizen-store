# D360: The status page

**The status page (wave 9 unit G, `docs/wave-9-platform.md` section 3, G); built.** `/status` and `/no/status` on Kaizena Flow's own site show five parts (`STATUS_COMPONENTS`, `src/lib/status.ts`): stores and websites (the database read behind every page), sign-in (Supabase Auth's health), payments (a call to Stripe with the platform's key), email (of the last hour's emails tried, at least five, more than one in five failed is failing) and background work (`jobsNeedingLook()`: a job failing, or
the dispatcher late). `runStatusChecks()` (`src/server/status.ts`, in the five-minute dispatcher) asks each probe within five seconds and keeps a row of `commerce.status_checks` (passed or not, a reason code, never a secret or a person's data) for each part it could measure; a part with nothing set up keeps none and the page says *Not measured*, never up, as a five-minute slot with no check is drawn missing, never passed.
Each part has a 90-day bar (`dayBars()`: a day all passed, some failed, most failed or none ran) and its uptime over the checks that ran (`uptimeBasisPoints()`, never rounded up to 100 while one failed), and the page's headline is `overallState()` of the parts and open incidents; read through `statusOverview()`, cached a minute under `STATUS_TAG`, which each run and each incident refreshes.
When a part's newest two checks failed after one that passed (`alertDue()`), each platform admin is emailed once (`platform.status_alert`, keyed by the check).
Incidents (`status_incidents`, impact minor, major or maintenance, the parts affected) and their updates (`status_incident_updates`, never changed; the status investigating, identified, monitoring or resolved) are platform admins' words in English and, where given, Norwegian, posted at Platform → Status page and audited `platform.status_incident_*`; each is emailed to confirmed subscribers in their language (`platform.status_incident`, with `List-Unsubscribe`) and is an entry of the Atom feed
(`/status/feed.atom`, `/no/status/feed.atom`). Subscribing (`status_subscribers`) needs no account: an address gets a link (its token kept as a hash, a day) to a page with a button, three asks a day an address, the same answer either way; every email carries the address's own link to leave, which deletes the row, and one never confirmed goes after a week. Checks are kept 90 days. Error tracking in an EU-hosted tool is not part of it (the wave's decision 6: the company's choice).
`platform.hosting-cdn-uptime-status-page` stays partial. Since 30 October 2026 (PSI PERF-02) the website probe also times the admin's sign-in page fetched from Dublin, to its last byte, in `status_checks.duration_ms` (null when it did not answer 200 in time; never a failure of the website), and Platform → Usage and limits shows its median and slowest 5 % over 7 days (`adminFromDublin()`).
Migration versions: `20261028130914_status_page` (applied by CI on 28 October 2026, run 699; advisors to check), `20261030183334_status_check_duration` (applied by CI run 783; advisors INFO only). 1 November 2026 (PSI AST-10): a sixth part, `ai`, the platform's own model answering `testAi()` once a day (an hour after a failure, `aiCheckDue()`), objective 95 %; migration `20261101013029_status_ai` widens `status_checks_component`.
