# D901: Pipedrive: beside it, moving from it, and past it (plan)

**Pipedrive: keep it, move from it, or never need it (agreed 25 November 2026, `docs/pipedrive.md`; nothing of it built yet).** One engine serves three promises:

* **Keep Pipedrive.** Customers, companies, orders and requests are sent to the owner's Pipedrive. A deal won there becomes a quote here,
  ready to send. A *Kaizena Flow* panel inside Pipedrive shows what the person bought.
* **Move to Kaizena Flow.** Connect, Check, Copy, both kept in step, a guided switch day, 30 days of watching, and Undo while nothing new
  depends on the copy.
* **Past Pipedrive.** Thirty parity units (P1 to P30) for a team that never had Pipedrive.

Every connection goes through Kaizena Flow's own public Pipedrive app, which replaces the pasted API token (the token stays, for a move
only). Changes here are read from the event log with a cursor of the engine's own. Changes in Pipedrive arrive by its webhooks, plus a
nightly sweep. Every record links ours to theirs. A create whose answer was lost is looked up before any retry. Each field has one owning
side. The engine keeps to Pipedrive's daily budget, and every failure is a sentence for the owner. Whatever comes here goes through its
table's own writer.

**The owner's choices** (the assessment's nine questions: "go with your recommendations, however focus on versatility, flexibility,
reliability and ease of use"):

1. Both directions, here → Pipedrive and the panel first.
2. A public app.
3. Free and in the core; the move turns Deals on.
4. Contacts without an email allowed (P1).
5. Human Web's account as the first real check.
6. Microsoft 365 before Gmail.
7. Nobody subscribed by a move, with one confirmed *still want our emails?* message offered.
8. HubSpot after, on the same engine.
9. Pipedrive's hosting shown and said once at Connect, never blocking.

The plan takes over S7 of `docs/own-books-and-sales.md` and the Pipedrive half of its pilot exit (section 10.3 of the plan).
