# D897: A recipe's first words in the store's voice, written after Start (email plan M3.10)

Date: 2026-11-25

**Homer's first flow sounds like his store (25 November 2026, the owner: "continue").** The usability test (M3.10,
`docs/audits/email-marketing-usability-2026-11.md`) met pass mark 1, a flow on in four presses, with one gap: a recipe's emails start with
the ready-made words until the owner presses *Write with AI*. Writing them at *Start* would put an AI turn on a press Homer expects to be
instant.

- *Start* still makes the flow from the recipe and opens it at once. When the store has a text model and the words are the recipe's ready-made
  ones (not the owner's own automation's, which stay), the rewrite runs after the answer (`after()`, `writeRecipeInVoice()`): each email
  through the store's writer, as *Write with AI* on the whole flow does (`suggestBroadcast()`: its facts, brand and rules, told who the email
  reaches and when), in the store's first language, products picked per reader kept.
- **The owner's change wins.** It is saved as the flow's next version only while the flow is still as *Start* made it (`saveFlow(…,
  { onlyAtVersion: 1 })`: the first version, off and untested). An owner who edited it meanwhile keeps their words; a writer that cannot
  write leaves the ready-made ones.
- The flow's page says so (`?voice=1`): "Writing these emails in your store's voice" while it is the first version, then "Written in your
  store's voice". Nothing sends: the flow stays off until tested and switched on.
- Pass mark 2's gap (a sentence with its own terms) closed with `create_flow` (D889), which builds a flow from the owner's sentence.

Idle cost: nothing; one writer call per email of a recipe when the owner presses Start with AI set up.
