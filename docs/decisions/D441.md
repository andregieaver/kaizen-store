# D441: Apps for many stores

**Apps for many stores (wave 14 unit D, `docs/wave-14-developer-platform.md` D); D1, D2 and D3 built: apps, installing them, their events, removing them, and reviewing a listing.** *Developer mode:* an account accepts the developer terms (`commerce.developers`, the version `DEVELOPER_TERMS_VERSION`; the terms are a draft at `/docs/developers/apps/developer-terms` for legal review) at Account → Developer (`/admin/account/developer`, beside the account's other pages rather than the spec's
`/admin/developer`), then makes up to 25 apps. *An app* (`commerce.apps`, `src/server/developer-apps.ts`, its only writer) is an OAuth client of unit B's server with `kind = 'app'`: a client ID `kfapp_…`, a secret `kfas_…` kept as its SHA-256 and shown once (Renew), its name, logo, homepage, privacy policy, support email, HTTPS redirect URIs, the scopes it asks for (unit B's, plus the Admin API's powers but `webhooks:write`, `APP_SCOPES`: the endpoints' operations are the owner's alone, and
an app is sent its own events in D2), its distribution (`private`: only stores its developer works in; `unlisted`; `listed`, which asks for Kaizena Flow's review, D3) and its events address and topics with an events secret `kfws_…` kept encrypted and shown once (sent from D2). *Installing* is unit B's flow with the app's client and `resource=https://kaizenaflow.com/api/admin/v1` (only an app may ask for it, and an app for nothing else): the install screen shows the app, its host and verified
mark, what it asks for in words (the powers as the token screen words them) and one store to choose, offered only where the person holds every permission the app asks for (a power rests on the one permission its operations need, `POWER_PERMISSION`, and asking for the power asks for that permission too, since none of its operations runs without it; not on every operation's: `price_lists.set_prices` is a `products:publish` operation needing `customers:write`, which would keep a catalogue app from
stores where its person may not change customers) and, for a private app, only on its developer's stores; no level to choose.
`approveInstallation()` makes the installation: a grant with `resource = 'admin'` and `store_id` its one store, the app's scopes, `access = 'change'`, one live per app and store (`oauth_grants_app_store_key`; installing again ends the old one with its tokens); the token endpoint takes the app's `client_secret_basic`. *Its tokens* (`kfa_`) work on the Admin API (`apiCaller()`: the person's account active, a member of the store now, the second step where the store or a platform admin needs it, the
membership capped by the grant's scopes; each operation allowed only when the grant holds its permission and, for a power, the power, `appMayCall()`, and the person may do it now, `memberCan()`; budget `api` per installation; `Idempotency-Key` in `api_app_idempotency_keys`; audited as `App: {name}`) and on the owner MCP (`oauthAccess()` takes an installation there too).
The Admin API's 401 names its protected resource metadata (`/.well-known/oauth-protected-resource/api/admin/v1`). *Screens:* Settings → Apps and API lists *Installed apps* apart from the assistants, with *Remove* (owners; `removeInstallation()`, `oauth.app_removed`); an app's page shows how many stores it is on, never which. *D2 built: events and removing.* Each installation of an app with an events address has its own endpoint (`webhook_endpoints.app_grant_id`, `src/server/app-endpoints.ts`):
the app's address and secret (the same sealed text), the topics it chose that the installation's scopes read (a subject's read area, `SUBJECT_READ_AREA`) and `customer.erased` always; it follows the app's changes at once and is kept out of every owner's screen, count, workflow and Admin API call.
However an installation ends (Remove, a password or second step changed, the app blocked, the person who installed it leaving the store or erasing their account), a deferred trigger switches its endpoint off (`uninstalled`) and queues `app.uninstalled` now and `store.erase_requested` in 48 hours, which the sender sends though the endpoint is off; nothing else is sent, and an owner is never emailed about an app's failing endpoint.
Installing again moves the endpoint to the new installation and cancels a waiting `store.erase_requested`. The two topics (receivers `apps`, subject `installation`) are contract 1.7. When an app asks for more, the store's Apps screen says *Asks for more* and an owner gives it with *Give it this* (`approveInstallationScopes()`, `oauth.app_scopes_approved`) while the installer may give it all.
Erasing an account blocks its apps and removes its developer row. *D3 built: listing and the docs.* Saving an app as *Listed* asks for Kaizena Flow's review (again after a decline); Platform → App listings (`/admin/platform/apps`) approves it with an optional note or declines it with the reason, both emailed to the developer in their admin's language (`platform.app_listing_decided`) and shown on the app's page, audited as `platform.app_listing_approved` or `platform.app_listing_declined`.
The directory's read (`directoryApps()`) is empty while `APP_DIRECTORY_OPEN` is off, and its page waits for the legal texts' approval: a route at `/apps` would hide any page of Kaizena Flow's own site at that address while showing nothing. The data processing terms and the review checklist are drafts for legal review beside the developer terms.
The Admin API's OpenAPI document names the apps' OAuth flow (`appOAuth`, each operation with the permission and power an app's token needs; none on the owner's webhook endpoints), contract 1.8. Docs: *A first app* (a Node script) and the owners' *Apps from other companies* (English and Norwegian). Migration `app_events`; tests in `commerce.test.ts` and `apps.int.test.ts`.
An installation is never copied with a store (`COPY_RULES`, a secret table) nor exported (`secret`): it has its own tokens and acts as one person of the store. Migration `apps`. Tests: `apps.test.ts`, `apps.int.test.ts`, and `commerce.test.ts` (an installation names its one store, one live per app and store; one app per client).

Date: 2026-11-04

Asked for by the owner (wave 14, unit D). *Why an installation is a grant of one store:* unit B's grant is one per person and client across their stores; an app is the store's integration, installed and removed per store, so each installation has its own tokens and budget and one store's owner removes it without touching another's. *Why the app gets exactly what it asked for:* an app is built against its scopes; a person who cannot give them all is not offered the store, rather than installing
an app that fails half its calls. *Why the powers ride on the grant:* D440's review asked that a power be checked against the person's permission at every call; the route already asks each operation's permission of the person, and the grant adds its own cap. *Why the developer page sits under Account:* every page an account has for itself is there, in its menu.
