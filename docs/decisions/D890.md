# D890: Which companies visited: company recognition on the visit counter, through Leadfeeder

Date: 2026-11-24

**Which companies visited (24 November 2026, the owner: "which companies have visited a store, with a frequency timeline and other company
insights", as Vainu and Leadfeeder show).** A store that counts visits can see which companies' own networks its visitors came from, how
often they came back and what they looked at. Kaizena Flow's own store first (`docs/analytics.md` "Companies").

- **The source is Leadfeeder's IP Enrich API** (`GET https://api.leadfeeder.com/v1/ip/enrich`, `X-Api-Key`, Kaizena Flow's account; AWS
  Ireland, a data processing agreement concluded with the account). Its terms let a platform build the data into its own product and show
  it to its customers, and it bills only the addresses it names as a company. **RIPE's register was considered and refused**: its terms
  (Article 4) forbid "advertising, direct marketing, marketing research or similar purposes" and downloading or compiling more than an
  insubstantial part without RIPE NCC's permission. Snitcher (Frankfurt, "from $10,000 a year", resale only by a separate agreement), Albacross
  (keeps the addresses it is sent for its own purposes, IPv4 only) and the downloadable databases (IPinfo, MaxMind: US, an OEM licence each)
  were weighed and left.
- **It rides on visit counting.** A page view `recordVisit()` counted, on a store with `stores.company_recognition` on, is handed to
  `recordCompanyVisit()` after the beacon is answered (`after()`, loaded only for such a store). Robots and visitors sending Global Privacy
  Control or Do Not Track are never counted, so never looked up. No cookie, no storage, no banner.
- **The address is never kept.** It is sent to Leadfeeder only, and the day's answer is kept for two days under a keyed hash of the UTC day
  and the address (`company_lookups`), so an address is asked about once a day whatever the store; no statement is ever given the address.
  What is kept is the company's facts once (`visit_companies`, platform-wide) and a store's day for it (`company_visits`: visitor-days,
  page views, the page shapes `placeOfPath()` keeps, at most 30), deleted with the visits after 25 months. A company is never tied to a
  visit row, a cart, an order or a customer.
- **Never a person.** A shared network (home, mobile, university, coworking) is not named. A sole trader, by its legal form in eleven
  languages or, for a Norwegian one, Brønnøysund's organisation form `ENK`, is never kept: its company is a person. Leadfeeder's
  `do_not_contact` is shown on the company's card.
- **Bounded cost.** At most 2,000 lookups a store a day the day's answers do not hold (`LOOKUPS_PER_STORE_DAY`); a failure is kept for the
  day too, so a broken service is not asked on every page view.
- **Homer**: Analytics → Companies lists the companies, most recently seen first, each with its industry, size, place and website, a bar
  per day of the period and the pages it looked at. Analytics → Settings has one switch. **Lisa**: the same card says what it can and cannot
  see; a platform admin saves the Leadfeeder key (kept encrypted, shown by its hint) and account id there.
- **Platform first, stores next.** Only Kaizena Flow's own store (`house`) may switch it on, since the cost is Kaizena Flow's; its site's
  cookie page says it while it is on. Opening it to every store (its price in the plans, a store's own key) is the next step.

Idle cost: none while the switch is off; while on, one cached read per counted page view and a Leadfeeder call per new address a day.
