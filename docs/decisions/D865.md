# D865: A lead score for business customers, in code with each point's reason (email plan M2.8)

Date: 2026-11-21

**A business customer's page says how warm they are as a lead, out of 100, with each point's reason (21 November 2026, email plan
M2.8).** Staff selling to businesses could see a customer's deals, quotes and emails on the timeline, but nothing weighed them: which
company to call first was a guess.

- The score is only `leadScore()` (`src/lib/lead-score.ts`), pure code, never a model: a quote asked for in the last 90 days and not
  declined, 25 each up to 50; an open deal, 20; a press on a link in the store's emails this month, 10 each up to 20, else one in the
  last 3 months, 5; a product looked at while signed in, 5 each up to 15. Capped at 100, it reads Hot from 60, Warm from 25, Cool above 0.
  Each reason is shown with its count and points, largest first.
- Visits count only as those product views: the store keeps no visit per person (a visit is a daily hash, D303), and product views are kept
  only for signed-in customers who allowed marketing cookies, a week, while a browse email runs (D691). No opens are counted (M0.1 b).
- One statement reads the facts (`customerLeadScore()`, `src/server/lead-score.ts`). A customer who buys for no company (no company name and
  no company account, D108) has no score and no card. Deals count only while Deals is on.
- The words are an area of their own (`leadScore`, server-only), so the pages that already reach the customers area through other
  imports do not carry them.
- Kaizena Flow's own customers are its house store's (D467), whose customer pages get the same card: nothing platform-only is needed.
- The score is a segment condition too (`{"kind": "leadScore", "min": n}`, API 1.53): `leadScoresByEmail()` reads every business customer
  through the same statement as the page, so a flow, broadcast or campaign can choose warm leads with the score their pages show. It is
  offered while the store sells to businesses (`business`).

Idle cost: one statement more on a business customer's page, and one per segment read whose rules name the score; none elsewhere.
