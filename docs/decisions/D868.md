# D868: Split tests in broadcasts and flows, judged by the A/B engine (email plan M2.5)

Date: 2026-11-21

**Split tests in broadcasts and flows are designed before they are built (21 November 2026, email plan M2.5), in
`docs/split-tests.md`.** The plan's row asked for subject, words, send time or a branch, judged by D148's engine with unsubscribes and
complaints as guardrails, a broadcast's winner going to the rest.

- The engine judges: `callRates()` with a new unit, `recipient`, its interval rule, split check and floor. Code counts and calls; a model
  never declares a winner.
- A broadcast has at most two versions; a share of the audience (20 % by default) is split between them, by a keyed hash of the subscriber
  and broadcast, kept on each recipient's row; the rest are held and, after the wait (4 hours by default), get the version more people
  pressed, or the first when the difference is not clear. Presses are the default goal, orders the other.
- A version whose unsubscribes or complaints are clearly higher never wins. A test is offered only when the tested share reaches the floor
  in each version (2,000 readers at 20 %); below that it says how many more it needs.
- Flows get a `split` step; the report says which way earns more; keeping the better way is the owner's press, a new tested version.
- Send time is left to M4.4 (send time per person). Kaizena Flow's own broadcasts and flows run in its house store, so they get the same.
- Five slices, the first two with one migration (`broadcast_tests`), the fourth with another (`flow_split`).

Idle cost: none until a test runs; then one job per due test after its wait.
