# storefront-client: What a shopper's browser loads

A shopper's first page is mostly well kept, and nothing here argues for removing a feature: a cold home page sends 680 KB of scripts (213 KB gzip), 63 % of it React and Next, with no third-party script and no font file. What is left is cost that does not follow use and that grows with page views once stores open. On a default store every document load makes two extra function calls (a wishlist read that is nearly always empty and, on pages with a product grid, a stock read for tiles that show nothing), carries about 48 KB raw / 16 KB gzip of admin words, catalogues and one unused library in its first scripts, and sends the store's layout twice (25 KB) with the store's 404 page inside. Eleven working routes share one 54-module bundle (30 KB gzip more than a built page), stores with several languages prefetch 70 to 105 KB of views a press never opens, and a store in an AI-translated language would send its whole interface text, about 100 KB, in every page. Thirteen findings: six are small and safe (P0); the two that move route files (per-route bundles, the drawer's slot) are cheapest before opening; the budgets that should have caught the growth have about 150 KB of room and cover two storefront routes.

- Product tiles in content grids are plain anchors, never prefetched (src/components/content-grid.tsx). The language switcher part (LanguageLinks) draws plain anchors too, so it never prefetches (src/components/language-links.tsx:84).

- Weighed and left: the browser texts of all four languages load together (24,831 raw / about 7.2 KB gzip). docs/performance.md:52-60 (D212) records that a per-language load made the page wait and lost presses.
- Weighed and left: filter-dialog (11,773 raw), site-form (6,348) and carousel (6,712) are in every built page's first scripts; the written rule keeps what is pressed before its script runs (docs/performance.md:38-51).
